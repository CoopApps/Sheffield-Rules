# The Youdan Cup

## Overview

**Definition**: The earliest adult football knockout trophy, competed for by Sheffield clubs in 1867.

The Youdan Cup was held 4 years before the FA Cup was launched (1871). While Charles Alcock claimed the FA competition was based on the Cock House tournament at Harrow School, the success of the Youdan Cup may have influenced his thinking.

**Key Facts**:
- First held: 1867
- Only staged for 1 year
- World's first adult football club knockout competition
- Preceded the FA Cup by 4 years

## Background Context

### Sheffield and London - An Imperfect Couple

#### Harry Walker Chambers and the FA Connection

**Harry Chambers** was a member of Sheffield Football Club and former secretary who played a crucial role in the relationship between Sheffield and London football.

**Timeline of Events**:

**October 1863** - Formation of the FA
- Chambers possibly present at gatherings leading to the FA's formation
- Was studying for law exams in London at the time
- Had been educated in London previously
- His father's practice used London agents Pitman and Lane (near King's Walk and Morley)
- May have mentioned to Ebenezer Cobb Morley that Sheffield clubs played to a standard code

**November 1863** - Sheffield FC Joins the FA
- Sheffield Football Club became members of the FA
- This decision would eventually lead to the demise of their superior standing at home
- Sheffield appeared subordinate to the London association socially
- William Chesterman (club secretary) wrote to complain about hacking in draft FA rules

**December 1863** - FA Rules Refined
- Hacking and running with the ball removed
- New laws still contained elements contrary to Sheffield game
- Sheffield FC had responsibility to promote FA rules after joining
- Played fixtures out-of-town, possibly in missionary fashion for FA rules
- Could not be seen playing two types of game, so avoided Sheffield-based teams

**2 January 1864** - Exhibition Match
- Chambers selected to play in FA exhibition game
- Played for Arthur Pember's President's side vs. Morley's Secretary's side
- First game under FA rules with club representatives
- Dined at Grosvenor Hotel, Pimlico afterwards
- Likely realized more needed to be done in Sheffield to promote FA cause

#### Rules Experimentation

**1864-1865 Season**:
- Sheffield encouraged local clubs to experiment with FA offside rule
- FA offside: anyone forward of the ball was "off-his-side"
- Entirely contrary to accepted Sheffield game
- Hallam club advertised practice match to "try the new offside rule"
- Outcome: goalless games led to abandonment of FA rule

### Sheffield Rules vs. London Rules

#### 22 February 1866 - FA Annual General Meeting

**Chesterman's Letter** (read by EC Morley):
- Sheffield couldn't send delegate but had no objection to proposed alterations
- Glad the fair catch clause would be expunged
- Their rules were nearly the same as FA rules
- Both decidedly against hacking, throttling, or wrestling
- **Suggested advisability of Sheffield playing a picked London team**

**FA Rule Changes (February 1866)**:

1. **Touch Downs Introduced**
   - Could be obtained either side of goal post at any point along goal line
   - Sheffield rules had "rouges" - grounding ball between rouge flags 4 yards apart either side of 4-yard goal posts

2. **Offside Rule Modified**
   - New: Three defenders had to be between attacker and goal line
   - Allowed modicum of forward passing
   - More aligned with Sheffield rule
   - Compromise aimed at London public school players, not Sheffield

3. **Throw-In Rule**
   - First player to touch ball awarded possession
   - Throw had to be at right angles to touch line
   - Had to touch ground before another player could play ball

   **Sheffield Rule 11 (October 1858)**:
   > "A ball in touch is dead, consequently the side that touches it down, must bring it to the edge of the touch, and throw it straight out at least six yards from touch."

   - Sheffield had 6-yard minimum to prevent struggle for possession
   - FA rule had no such clause, making restarts difficult

**Sheffield's Strategy**:
- Chambers and Chesterman hoped for return game in Sheffield
- Could demonstrate Sheffield style of play
- Coax Londoners into their way of thinking

## 31 March 1866 - The London Match

### The Game

**Location**: Battersea Park

**Significance**:
- First representative match ever played by the Football Association (Geoffrey Green)
- "Deserves a place of honour" for what it led to in international relationships

**Purpose**:
- To test out the new association rules (Keith Booth)

**Controversy**:
- Sheffield team possibly unhappy, believing they'd been misled over which rules would apply
- London may have been confused about who Chambers and Chesterman represented
- Believed they spoke for all football organizations in Sheffield
- Initial invite stated "representative team of Sheffield clubs"

**Teams**:

*London Team*:
- Drawn from four FA clubs
- Arthur Pember (FA President)
- Ebenezer Cobb Morley (FA Secretary)
- Charles William Alcock (future international)
- Arthur Kinnaird (future international and FA President)

*Sheffield Team*:
- Actually just Sheffield Football Club
- Some players played for other clubs (e.g., John Charles Shaw was captain of Hallam Club)

**Result**:
- Convincing win for London
- Sheffield failed to score
- Match noted for physical and robust style of play

### The Albion Hotel Dinner

**Location**: Covent Garden, Albion Hotel on Russell Street

**Attendees**:
- Both teams dined together after the match
- Formal seating arrangement likely

**Seating**:
- Top table: Chesterman, Chambers, Pember, Morley, possibly Kinnaird
- Long tables with mixed seating for interaction
- Possible pairing: Alcock and Shaw (both had established own clubs, would achieve authority in their associations)

**Topics of Discussion**:
- Speeches and toasts centered on football
- Unification of clubs under same rules
- Situation likely emerged that Sheffield players all belonged to same club
- Chambers and Chesterman possibly chastised for misrepresenting their position
- May have been pointed out that other Sheffield teams hadn't experienced London rules
- Other Sheffield teams didn't have a voice

**Outcome**:
- Return match at Sheffield agreed for March 1867
- Sheffield had a year to "put their house in order"

## The Youdan Competition

### Motivation and Timing

**The FA Rebuke**:
- The rebuke regarding an immediate return game may have galvanized Chambers and Chesterman into radical action
- Sheffield FC was the premier club, members considered themselves elite
- Their authority could not be questioned - they had forged the Sheffield rules and were guardians
- **Need**: Draw all existing Sheffield clubs into a recognizable form accepted by London - a functioning association
- **Timing**: Preferably before the next FA meeting

**Origin of the Idea**:
- Murphy claims it was entirely Thomas Youdan's concept
- Westby supports this, calling it Youdan's marketing idea
- More likely: During months following Battersea Park game, Chambers and Chesterman organized a committee
- Committee ensured agreement and framework for competition
- Required co-operation from clubs represented on committee

**Possible Inspiration - Dodworth Cricket Tournament**:
- June 1866: Knockout tournament at Dodworth (near Barnsley) for a silver cup
- Only short distance from Sheffield
- Noted by Davies and Light in *Cricket and Community in England* (1867)
- Local press reports: Silkstone village won silver cup in knockout competition during Dodworth feast celebrations
- Part of local cricket culture - many football players belonged to cricket fraternity
- Could have provided catalyst for Youdan Cup

### The Youdan Committee

**Formation**: 28 January 1867
- Confirmed by *Sporting Life*: "A crowded meeting of representatives of 13 football clubs of Sheffield and neighbourhood was held on Monday evening at the Adelphi Hotel to make arrangements for competing for a valuable silver cup"
- Almost certain Sheffield Football Club representatives were at the helm

**Known Committee Members**:
- **John Charles Shaw** (Hallam FC) - member
- **Frederick Corbett** - Honorary Secretary
  - Originally from London
  - On committee of Norfolk club
  - Name appears in block adverts regarding tournament progress

**Committee Activities**:

*January 1867*:
- Corbett placed advertisement "To Artists and Designers"
- Offered cash prize for best trophy design
- Winning design selected by committee

**Committee's Achievement**:
- No official governing body existed in Sheffield
- Could not call it "Sheffield Association Challenge Cup"
- No funds to promote competition
- Still managed to organize comprehensive tournament structure

### Tournament Structure and Rules

**Participating Clubs** (12 teams):
1. Hallam
2. Heeley
3. Norton
4. Mechanics
5. Norfolk
6. Fir Vale
7. Broomhall
8. Pitsmoor
9. MacKenzie
10. Garrick
11. Milton
12. Wellington

**Notable Absence**: Sheffield Football Club did not participate
- Possible reasons:
  - Had already experienced playing under London rules
  - As guardians of Sheffield rules, their job was to oversee changes
  - May not have wanted to be defeated by another Sheffield side

**Tournament Rules**:

1. **Team Size**: Games to be played 12-a-side

2. **Punctuality**: Game to commence punctually at 3 o'clock. No waiting for players allowed.

3. **Duration and Tiebreakers**:
   - Game played for one hour and thirty minutes in usual way
   - If draw at end of time: clubs toss for choice of goals and play on
   - First point scored decides game
   - If still drawn after one hour's extra play: adjourned to Monday at 3 o'clock

4. **Officials**: Two umpires and one referee per match
   - Umpires provided by participating clubs
   - Neutral referees appointed
   - **Referee's decision is final** (unique feature for football at the time)
   - Introduced to avoid disputes
   - **John Tomlinson** (leading Broomhall official) took charge of semi-final and final
   - FA adopted this strategy for Challenge Cup latter stages in 1871

**Additional Rule Amendments**:
- After first round: Committee introduced clause giving referees power to award free kicks
- Addressed infringements possibly resulting from not having Sheffield six-yard rule for throws

### Rules Experimentation

**Evidence of FA Rules Testing**:

1. **Offside Rule**:
   - Games were not free scoring, particularly after first round
   - Suggests possibility of London offside rule being used

2. **Throw-In**:
   - Right-angled throw with no distance regulation from touch appears to have been applied
   - Caused issues requiring mid-tournament free kick rule amendment

3. **Rouge Method Retained**:
   - Sheffield playing rule for differentiated scoring kept for tournament
   - Proved particularly valuable during latter stages

**Strategic Purpose**:
- Chambers and Chesterman could either:
  - Go back to London and accept London rules (with backing of Sheffield clubs)
  - Persuade FA to amend rules in Sheffield's favour

### Thomas Youdan - The Sponsor

**Background**:
- Owned Alexandra Music Hall on Blonk Street
- Well known to town's footballers - favourite port of call
- Regarded as a maverick
- Used every marketing ploy to entice public to his establishment
- Known for philanthropy in promoting local causes
- Donated monies for cups and prizes

**Role**:
- **Did not create tournament structure** - merely provided sponsorship
- Likely approached by committee member with idea
- Benefits for Youdan:
  - Linked with popular tournament
  - Welcome revenue driver
  - **Early example of commercial sponsorship**

### Financial Arrangements

**Player Welfare Fund**:
- Weekly fund to support players injured in games who could not work
- One injured player awarded five shillings during competition
- Harvey mentions accident fund: players paid in, received eight shillings per week when unfit for work
- Almost certainly established for season following competition
- May have been one of the enticements to participate

**Replay Scheduling**:
- Monday scheduled for replays in event of tie
- Example: Hallam vs. Norfolk second round game was drawn, replayed on Monday
- "Saint Monday" (unofficial holiday) still strong in Sheffield working culture

**The Theft**:
- Vast sum of £70 stolen from Thomas Youdan's offices (£8000 by 2023 standards)
- Westby claims this money was raised during tournament
- Local press reports: theft occurred evening of 1 March 1867
- **Before** gate monies taken for final (5 March) and second place game (9 March)
- Money may have had no link with football
- Harvey reports eventual gate money amounted to £50

**Financial Questions**:
- How did committee arrange funding for injured players?
- What happened to gate money?
- Possibility: clubs paid into fund prior to competition
- Proviso: any monies raised would return to clubs

### Sheffield Football Players' Accident Society

**Establishment**:
- Direct "spin-off" from Youdan competition
- Injuries more frequent and common during tournament games
- Indicated growing seriousness of competition
- May indicate new set of rules being applied

**Why More Injuries?**:

*Sheffield Game vs. London Rules*:
- Alcock described Sheffield game as "loose and disjointed"
- Directly encouraged passing with players dispersed over field
- Battersea Park game noted for physicality due to FA rules
- Led to game of attrition between forward players
- Injuries almost inevitable

**Evidence from Match Reports**:

*MacKenzie vs. Hallam (at Bramall Lane, Saturday)*:
> "The Mackenzie and Hallam football clubs competed for this prize at Bramall lane on Saturday, and some good play was the result, although it was evident from the commencement that Hallam had the advantage, their side being a much more bulky specimen of humanity than their opponents."

Critical observation:
> "Hallam certainly showed the best play but there was far too much playing the men instead of the ball, as there generally is in these matches thus giving the advantage to heavy rough over the lighter players."

**Question**: What did reporter mean by "these matches"?
- Football in general?
- Games played in Youdan competition under London rules?

**Subsequent Season**:
- Benefit society for players formalized under Sheffield Football Association
- Players contributed through clubs
- Monies not enough for amount of claims
- Fundraising events staged to supplement benefit fund
- Thomas Youdan assisted with evenings at Alexandra Music Hall dedicated to fund
- Town vs. Country football match staged at Bramall Lane

### Tournament Format: True Knockout

**False Claims**:
- Westby, Harvey, and Murphy claimed competition was not true knockout
- Claimed Norfolk achieved bye to final
- Claimed it became three-way competition
- **These claims are incorrect**

**Reality**:
- **Was a knockout competition throughout**
- Only after the final did committee agree to second place game
- Reason for second place game: final attracted crowd over 3000
  - Charging 3d each
  - Ladies admitted free
  - Clear financial incentive

**Schedule**:
- **First round**: Saturday, 16 February 1867
- **Second round**: One week later (23 February 1867)
- **Final**: 5 March 1867
- **Second place game**: 9 March 1867 (4 days after final)

## Formation of Sheffield Football Association

### The February-March 1867 Meetings

**12 February 1867** - Chesterman's London Visit (per Percy Young):
- Travelled to London to amend certain rules to fit Sheffield style of play
- Unsuccessful
- Reception recorded in FA minute book

**26 February 1867** - FA Annual General Meeting:
- Brown maintains meeting actually occurred this date (not 12 February)
- Chesterman present
- Letter read from Chambers of Sheffield Football Association (now President)

**Robert Willis (FA Hon. Secretary) read letter**:
> "Mr Chambers of the Sheffield Association stated that the association represented 14 clubs and 1000 or 1200 members and whatever was done that evening by the meeting would receive every attention at the hands of the Sheffield players."

**Context**:
- Before Youdan competition commenced, loose association of clubs had emerged
- Almost certainly formed to oversee Youdan competition
- This AGM was watershed for FA fortunes
- Numbers dwindled to six clubs
- Morley considered disbanding
- Sheffield letter had impact (though not regarding rule amendments)

**Chesterman's Statement** (per Brown):
> "Mr Chesterman said the alterations the Sheffield men proposed were not of much consequence, as they would be guided by the results of that night's deliberations, giving all their attention to the superior alterations that the London representatives might make."

**Outcome**:
- FA rejected Sheffield amendments
- Chesterman and Chambers had no option but to accept 1867 FA rules

### Pre-Final Meeting - Before 5 March 1867

**First Meeting** (adjourned):
- Held at Adelphi Hotel
- Proposed to accept London rules
- Met with resistance from clubs
- Meeting adjourned to give clubs time to consider rules and put forward amendments

### Post-Final Meeting - 6 March 1867

**Location**: Adelphi Hotel, Sheffield

**Chairman**: H.W. Chambers (President)

**Attendance**: "Very numerously attended, thirteen clubs being represented"

**Significance**: Date of Sheffield Football Association founding

**Reported by**:
- *The Sportsman* (14 March, Charles Alcock was journalist there)
- *Sporting Life* (13 March)
- *Sheffield and Rotherham Independent* (11 March)

**Proceedings**:

1. **Minutes Read and Confirmed**

2. **Committee Election**:
   - One representative from each club elected
   - Gave all clubs equal voice
   - **Democratic model established**

3. **Code of Laws Formation**:
   - Laws of General Association (FA) taken as proposition
   - Laws as played in Sheffield moved as amendments
   - Voting was close-run thing
   - Rules duly amended according to club wishes

**Prior Plans**:
- *Sheffield and Rotherham Independent* (11 March) reported:
  > "The return match between London and Sheffield is fixed for Saturday April 6th at Sheffield. The following are the rules of the Association for the season 1867-8, revised and corrected from the late alterations."
  > [Rules listed were FA rules in their entirety]

**What Happened**:
- Return match against London had been arranged
- After revised laws took shape, game was cancelled
- London would only play by their rules
- Sheffield now had own association on larger scale than London
- Could determine own direction within democratic model

**Harvey's Assessment**: "Embryonic Sheffield Football Association showed interest in adopting laws of FA" - understated; Sheffield men were desperate to force the issue, but clubs demanded democratic voice

## The Cromwell Cup (1868)

### Overview

**Sponsor**: Oliver Cromwell
- Stage manager of Theatre Royal
- Direct competition with Thomas Youdan's Alexandria Music Hall

**Format**:
- Minor knockout competition
- For organizations not exceeding 2 years of age
- Four clubs participated
- Played under existing Sheffield rules
- Teams played 14-a-side

2. **Wellington** - two seasons old

**Venue**: Bramall Lane

- 15 miles north of Sheffield
- According to Curry, area played significant part in development of modern club football in Sheffield

## Conclusion: Historical Significance

### The Youdan Cup's Place in History

**Dual Achievements**:
1. Earliest adult knockout tournament of its kind
2. Pre-dated FA Challenge Cup by four years
3. Galvanizing force that helped create first provincial association in country

**Complex Circumstances**:
- Interactions of individuals with different agendas
- Power struggles
- "Men made" set of circumstances worthy of scrutiny
- Helped shape and put together the modern game

### Social Dynamics

**Sheffield Football Club's Motivations** (Collins):
> "Emulation of their social superiors has long been a defining characteristic of the British middle classes and the Sheffield footballers were no exception."

**Timeline of Sheffield FC's London Alliance**:
- Eager to enhance social status by allying with FA
- FA comprised powerful individuals and groups from metropolis
- Became members November 1863
- Committed to promoting London cause
- Despite efforts to influence rules, bowed to collective superiority of London

**1866 Battersea Park Game**:
- More than just FA's first representative match
- Heralded beginnings of concerted effort to reconcile differences
- Sheffield FC needed to ensure metropolitan rules were understood and accepted by local teams

### Purpose of Youdan Cup

**Curry's Framework**: "Localism" giving way to "universalism"
- Both sides would accept compromise eventually
- But compromise was many years away

**Young's Assertion**:
> "It would ill become them as law-makers to sanction a team of theirs playing other rules"
- London was in ascendency

**Youdan Cup Purpose**:
- Narrow window of opportunity to involve Sheffield clubs
- Ensure metropolitan rules were understood
- Get local teams to accept FA rules
- Outcome: Creation of Sheffield Football Association

### Sheffield Football Association Formation

**Westby's Claim**: Sheffield FA began January 1867
- Might be correct
- But no democratic voice at this point
- FA rules were "forced" home

**Official Founding**: 6 March 1867
- Club representatives elected onto committee
- Rule structures debated and amended
- Democratic model established

### Leadership Transitions

**Harry Walker Chambers**:
- Did not stay long as President
- By 1869, replaced by John Charles Shaw

**John Charles Shaw**:
- Took position in 1869
- Held "chair" for fourteen years
- Oversaw rule changes
- Oversaw eventual amalgamation with London in 1877

**William Chesterman**:
- Remained on FA committee
- October 1871: Failed to be re-elected to London FA committee (out-of-London representative)
- 1872: Replaced by Walter Skinner (viewed as Shaw's man)
- By time Shaw became Sheffield FA President, Skinner was already honorary secretary

**New Power Dynamic**:
- With Chambers and Chesterman out of equation
- Shaw and Alcock became driving forces of respective organizations

### Why Only One Year?

**Youdan Cup**:
- Not held again, most likely because purpose had been served
- Collins (podcast): Youdan lost interest due to difficulty obtaining drinks licence
  - From William Clegg (aggressive teetotaller)
  - Father of John Charles Clegg (future FA President)

**Cromwell Cup**:
- Played next year involving four clubs
- Also only played once

### Impact on Cup Competitions

**Matthew Taylor**:
> "Crucial to the growth and survival of clubs and associations was the development of cup competitions"

**One Purpose Fulfilled**:
- Though Sheffield FA Challenge Cup emerged many years later

**Influence on Charles Alcock**:
- Taylor: "Would be surprising if Charles Alcock had not been inspired by the apparent success of the Youdan competition"

### The 1871 Return Match

**December 1871** - Long Awaited Return Game:
- Charles Alcock brought representative London side to play Sheffield at Bramall Lane
- **London side played Sheffield rules**
- London short one man - Shaw stepped in to make up numbers for London
- London's umpire was from Sheffield: Harry Walker Chambers
- Alcock played against wishes of London committee
- Knew they would be playing different code

**Post-Match Dinner**:
- Both teams dined at Adelphi Hotel
- Alcock (visiting captain) undoubtedly sat next to Shaw (Sheffield FA President)
- Like Battersea game, what happened afterward was significant
- Alcock possibly sought last-minute reassurances regarding framework of Youdan Cup's success
- Regarding his forthcoming FA Challenge Cup competition

**Timing**:
- October 1871: FA Challenge Cup launched
- This game played in December 1871

**The Rest is History**.
