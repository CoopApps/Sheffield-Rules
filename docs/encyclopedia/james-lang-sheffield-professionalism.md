# James J. Lang and Emergent Soccer Professionalism in Sheffield

## Overview

**Central Focus**: Career of James J. Lang, a Scotsman "seemingly invited to Sheffield with the sole intention of playing football" in the late 1870s and early 1880s

**Historical Significance**: Lang may have been "one of the first players to participate professionally" in association football

**Context**: Examines emergent professionalism within Sheffield football during a critical transitional period before professional football was officially accepted (1885)

**Three Areas of Social Tension**:
1. Payment for playing
2. Importation of players
3. Players representing multiple clubs

## Sheffield Football's Pioneering Achievements

### Institutional Innovations

**World's Oldest Football Club**: Sheffield FC (founded 24 October 1857)

**Oldest Existing Fixture**: Sheffield vs Hallam (first played 26 December 1860)

**First Inter-City Match**: Sheffield vs London (1866)

### Rule Innovations

1. **Corner kick**: Introduced by Sheffield legislators
2. **Fixed crossbar**: Sheffield innovation
3. **Free kick**: Sheffield introduction

### Competition Innovations

**First Provincial Cup Competition**: Established 1876

### Technological Innovation

**First Floodlit Football Match**: 1878 at Bramall Lane (Sheffield's most prominent sporting venue)

## Sheffield FC: The World's Oldest Football Club (1857)

### Foundation (24 October 1857)

**Originated From**: Local cricket club

**Leading Lights**:
- **Nathaniel Creswick**: Attended Sheffield Collegiate School (April 1839 - June 1847)
- **William Prest**: Originally from York, lived on Collegiate Crescent near the Collegiate, keen local sportsman

**Both**: Keen local sportsmen who initiated Sheffield FC

### Player Recruitment

**Primary Source**: "They invited most participants from the ranks of former pupils of Sheffield Collegiate School"

**Possible Influence**: "May have come under the influence of ex-public schoolmen in their introduction to the game"

### Rule Creation Process

**Evidence Suggests**: "Both Prest and Creswick decided to write to each public school requesting a set of rules, using the preferred points of each to decide on a set of regulations for Sheffield"

**Result**: Rules showed distinct characteristics

---

## Sheffield FC Rules: Anti-Rugby Bias

### Distinctive Features

**Bias**: "Showed a distinct bias towards the kicking style of the game"

**Two Key Features**:
1. **Limited handling**
2. **No hacking**

**Significance**: "Deliberately legislated away from the Rugby form"

**Can Be Described As**: "Markedly anti-Rugby in form"

---

## Debate: Public School Influence on Sheffield Football

### The Question

**Issue**: "Some debate has taken place as to how much influence former public schoolboys had on early Sheffield football"

**Little Doubt**: The 1857 Sheffield rules "can be described as markedly anti-Rugby in form"

---

## The Rouge: Evidence of Eton Influence (1861-62)

### Addition to Sheffield Rules

**Date**: 1861-62

**Innovation**: The "rouge"

**Source**: "A practice hitherto peculiar to the Eton Field Game"

**Significance**: "Must indicate some form of diffusion from that institution"

### Rouge in Competition

**Still Used**: 1866-67 season

**Decided Youdan Cup Final**: Hallam vs Norfolk
- **Result**: Hallam won by no goals and two rouges to no score
- First local Sheffield competition

**Implication**: Direct borrowing from Eton's Field Game rules

---

## Sheffield's Football Identity by Early 1860s

### Firmly in Soccer Camp

**Curry's Assessment**: "By the early 1860s Sheffield stood firmly in the camp of an embryonic soccer style of the game"

### Most Important Footballing Sub-Culture

**Bold Claim**: "It might be possible to argue that the footballers of the city had established the **most important footballing sub-culture in England** at this particular time"

**Context**: Early 1860s, before FA was established (1863)

**Significance**: Sheffield, not London, was the center of football development

---

## Hallam Football Club (1860)

### Foundation

**By 1860**: Sheffield FC had rivals in form of Hallam Football Club

**Route to Formation**: "Followed a similar route to other local teams...in growing from an existing cricket club"
- Sheffield United: Cricket club origin
- Sheffield Wednesday: Cricket club origin
- Pattern: Cricket clubs as foundation for football clubs

### Founders

**Captain Thomas Vickers** and **John Shaw**

**Motivation**: "Felt the need for a less exclusive footballing organization than that being offered at Sheffield FC"

**Implication**: Sheffield FC was perceived as exclusive/elite, Hallam more accessible

---

## The Oldest Existing Fixture (26 December 1860)

### First Sheffield vs Hallam Match

**Date**: Wednesday 26 December 1860

**Venue**: Hallam's Sandygate ground

**Status**: "The inter-club fixture generally regarded as the oldest still in existence"

---

## Hallam's Struggles: Location and Finance

### Geographic Disadvantage

**Problem**: Hallam "suffered from an inability to attract spectators to its Sandygate ground, which was some way from the city centre"

**Consequence**: Affected attendance and therefore gate receipts

### Impact on Player Recruitment

**Unable to Pay Players**: "Meant the club was perhaps unwilling though probably unable to pay players"

**Result**: "The best of whom drifted to other clubs"

**Significance**: By late 1870s/early 1880s, ability to pay players becoming important for attracting and retaining talent

## Theoretical Framework: Growing Competitiveness

### Eric Dunning's Analysis

**Identified Trend**: "Trend towards growing competitiveness, especially in high-level sport"

**Lang's Story**: "A good example of this trend"

### Erosion of Amateur Values

**Amateur Values**: Including "playing sport for no extrinsic reward"

**Being Replaced By**: Attitudes and structures that were "professional" in form

---

## The Nature of Historical Change

### Not Deliberate Planning

**Process**: "They were never intended, deliberate processes"

**Rather**: "'Blind' or 'unplanned'"

**Mechanism**: "The outcome of the fusion of the purposive actions of human beings interweaving over many generations"

**Implication**: Professionalism emerged gradually through many individual decisions, not through deliberate policy

---

## Three Areas of Social Tension in Early Football Professionalism

### Area 1: Payment for Playing

**Issue**: Whether players should receive money for participating

**Social Tension**: Violated amateur ethos

### Area 2: Importation

**Issue**: Bringing in players from outside the local area specifically to play football

**Lang's Role**: "An importation"

**Social Tension**: Perceived as unfair advantage, artificial strengthening of teams

### Area 3: Players Representing Multiple Clubs

**Issue**: Players appearing for more than one club

**Social Tension**: Questions of loyalty, fair competition
