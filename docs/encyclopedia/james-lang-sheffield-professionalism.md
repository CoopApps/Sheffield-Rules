# James J. Lang and Emergent Soccer Professionalism in Sheffield

## Overview

**Central Focus**: Career of James J. Lang, a Scotsman "seemingly invited to Sheffield with the sole intention of playing football" in the late 1870s and early 1880s

**Historical Significance**: Lang may have been "one of the first players to participate professionally" in association football

**Context**: Examines emergent professionalism within Sheffield football during a critical transitional period before professional football was officially accepted (1885)

**Three Areas of Social Tension**:
1. Payment for playing
2. Importation of players
3. Players representing multiple clubs

---

## Sheffield FC: The World's Oldest Football Club (1857)

### Foundation (24 October 1857)

**Originated From**: Local cricket club

**Leading Lights**:
- **Nathaniel Creswick**: Attended Sheffield Collegiate School (April 1839 - June 1847)
- **William Prest**: Originally from York, lived on Collegiate Crescent near the Collegiate, keen local sportsman

**Both**: Keen local sportsmen who initiated Sheffield FC

### Player Recruitment

**Primary Source**: "They invited most participants from the ranks of former pupils of Sheffield Collegiate School"

**Possible Influence**: "May have come under the influence of ex-public schoolmen in their introduction to the game"

### Rule Creation Process

**Evidence Suggests**: "Both Prest and Creswick decided to write to each public school requesting a set of rules, using the preferred points of each to decide on a set of regulations for Sheffield"

**Result**: Rules showed distinct characteristics

## Three Areas of Social Tension in Early Football Professionalism

### Area 1: Payment for Playing

**Issue**: Whether players should receive money for participating

**Social Tension**: Violated amateur ethos

### Area 2: Importation

**Issue**: Bringing in players from outside the local area specifically to play football

**Lang's Role**: "An importation"

**Social Tension**: Perceived as unfair advantage, artificial strengthening of teams

### Area 3: Players Representing Multiple Clubs

**Issue**: Players appearing for more than one club

**Social Tension**: Questions of loyalty, fair competition

---

## Areas of Tension in Emergent Professionalism

### Three Different Forms of Emergent Professionalism

**Curry's Analysis**: "It appears profitable to examine the trends of emergent professionalism in soccer, which, at this point, took on several different forms"

**Form 1**: Locally successful exponent supplementing income by selling services to highest bidder
- Example: William (Billy) Mosforth

**Form 2**: Importation of players from other regions or countries
- Examples: Arthur William Cursham (Nottingham to Sheffield), Scottish players migrating south

**Form 3**: Players representing multiple clubs
- Common practice in 1870s but creating tensions

## Billy Mosforth: Selling Services to Highest Bidder

### Background (1858-1929)

**Born**: Sheffield, 2 January 1858

**Died**: Sheffield, 11 July 1929

**Played**: Successfully in Sheffield area for many years

**Trade**: Engraver

**1881 Census**: Recorded as unmarried publican, keeping Royal Oak public house at 29 King Street (city centre)

### Club Career

**First Regular Appearances**: Albion club (Sheffield)

**Main Career**: Sheffield Wednesday (played majority of his career)

**Later**: Lockwood Brothers (works team formed by disaffected Wednesday players unhappy at club's stance against professionalism)

**Also Instrumental**: In forming Sheffield Rovers club, "an openly professional organization"

### International Career

**England Caps**: Nine international caps

**First Cap**: Age 19

**Significance**: "The only player in the team not to have attended university or public school"

**Status**: Outstanding athlete

### Earnings from Football

**Curry's Assessment**: "He probably earned a good deal of money from various sources in the game"

**But**: "Appears never to have been consistently rewarded for his efforts"

**Pattern**: Represented a number of different clubs, selling his services

## The Shirt-Changing Incident (11 October 1884)

### The Challenge (Autumn 1884)

**"A Hallam supporter"**: Writing to local newspaper

**Challenged Wednesday**: "Only play their own team, and not imports, in order to prove they are the best in Sheffield"

**Demand**: "When they meet Hallam, then it will be seen what their strength is"

**Context**: Challenge was "precursor to a game between the two clubs the following month"

### The Match: Wednesday vs Hallam (11 October 1884)

**Competition**: Sheffield Football Association (S.F.A.) Challenge Cup

**The Incident**:

> "Billy Mosforth almost had the crowd taking bets on which club he would play for. To encourage them further, Mosforth first appeared on the field in Hallam's colours, but on some pretext, returned to the dressing room and re-appeared in the Wednesday strip"

**Result**: Wednesday won the tie 5-1

### Alternative Account: The Bribe

**Another Writer's Details**:

> "He [Mosforth] was once stripped to play for Hallam against Wednesday, but, when a supporter called 'Ten bob and free drinks all week, Billy, if you'll change your shirt', he returned to the dressing room to re-emerge in Wednesday colours"

**"Ten bob"**: Ten shillings (substantial sum)

**Plus**: "Free drinks all week"

**Implication**: Mosforth openly bribed mid-match to switch teams

### Local Reaction

**Indignation**: "Such was the indignation locally"

**Gentleman Attending Game**: Described it as "an 'unprincipled piece of trafficking in professionalism on the part of Wednesday'"

**Significance**: Episode "openly suggest[s] that Mosforth received payment, and the general suspicion was that he earned a fairly substantial income from football"

### What the Incident Illustrates

**Two Key Points**:

1. **Players induced to play for highest bidder**: "How players would probably be induced to play for the highest bidder"

2. **Last-minute decisions**: "Their ability to decide, even at such a late stage, which team to represent"

---

## Mosforth vs Lang: Different Forms of Professionalism

### Mosforth's Pattern

**Locally Based**: "Almost certainly involved in selling his considerable football skills for money"

**But**: "He never transferred his allegiances to other geographical areas"

**Therefore**: "Not...a part of another form of emergent professionalism, that of **importation**"

**Significance**: Mosforth represents Form 1 (local player selling services), not Form 2 (importation)

## Importation: The Second Form of Emergent Professionalism

### Why Importation Especially Irked Football Enthusiasts

**Curry**: "This particular form especially irked the football enthusiast"

**Reasons**:
1. "Limited the chances of indigenous talent"
2. "Created negative opinions and tensions"

**What Seems Certain**: "Local footballers, administrators and spectators often **resented the importation of players** from other regions or countries"

**Especially When**: "Involved in competition for local prizes such as county or national cups"

## National Debates Over Scottish Importation

### The Athlete Correspondent

**Complaint**: "What honour can there be in a team of Scotchmen carrying the National Cup to any Lancashire village?"

**Context**: Resentment of Scottish players winning English trophies

### Athletic News: The Lancashire Cup Controversy

**Quote**:

> "I understood when I gave my mite towards purchasing the handsome cup [The Lancashire Football Association's trophy] that it was for **Lancashire lads, and they alone**. If the richer clubs can afford to pay professionals, let them do so, but when they compete for our grand trophy, let the **true Lancashire lads have equal chance** of winning it"

**Key Themes**:
- Cup intended for local players only
- "Richer clubs" paying professionals

## Arthur William Cursham Case (1877-1878)

### The Nottingham Player Who Played for Sheffield

**Who Was Cursham**: Nottingham-based England international

**What Sheffield FC Did**: "Secured the services" of Cursham for an FA Cup tie

**The Problem**: Sheffield's opponents in the tie were **Nottingham FC**

**Additional Problem**: Cursham "had already played regularly [for Nottingham] in friendly games in that same season"

### Legal But Controversial

**Perfectly Legal**: "The practice of representing one team in friendly fixtures whilst 'choosing' an entirely different club for cup ties was perfectly legal at the time"

**But Two Issues**:
1. "Importing a player from another district"
2. Player appearing against his regular club

### Was Money Involved?

**Curry's Assessment**: "It seems doubtful that Cursham's primer reason for preferring Sheffield to Nottingham was based on money"

**Why Doubtful**:
- Both clubs "controlled by upper middle class gentlemen"
- Both "generally thought to be upholders of amateur sporting values"
- Cursham's brother represented Corinthians FC (ex-public schoolboys and former university students)
- Indicates "family were of a relatively high social standing"

**But**: "The possibility of some kind of reward should not be dismissed entirely"

---

## The Two Matches: Sheffield vs Nottingham (1877-1878)

### First Match (FA Cup Tie)

**Result**: Draw

**Nottingham Reaction to Cursham**:

> "Cursham's appearance in the Sheffield XI elicited 'some slight expressions of disapprobation (which were quite improper) from the Nottingham people'"

**Reporter**: Sheffield newspaper noted the disapproval

**Significance**: Even when legal, practice was "much frowned upon in local soccer communities"

### Subsequent Friendly Matches

**December 1877**: Cursham lined up for Nottingham against Sheffield (friendly)

**February 1878**: Cursham again for Nottingham vs Sheffield (friendly)

**Irony**: Player who represented Sheffield in FA Cup tie against Nottingham, then played for Nottingham against Sheffield in friendlies

## Scottish Migration: "The Lure of English Gold"

### Why Scotsmen Moved South

**Primary Reason**: "Availability of non-football work as much as for football itself"

**Economic Pull**: "Opportunities for jobs at a higher rate of pay than that existing in their own country appear to have drawn them away"

**Football Factor**: "No doubt in many cases their extraordinary football skills created the initial opening"

### Athletic Journal Analysis

**Process Described**:

> "Such men 'serve their time to a trade at home, and when they are journeymen, and find trade bad, they come to England, where it is good'"

**Pattern**: Skilled tradesmen using football ability to secure better employment in England

### Scottish Writer's Perspective

**Description**: "The lure of English gold"

**Rhetorical Question**: "What Scotsman, after all, ever went to England without being paid for it?"

**Implication**: Payment for playing football was assumed, not exceptional

## Negative Attitudes Toward Scottish Importation

### The Athlete's Harsh Assessment

**Opinion**: "The 'employment of the scum of the Scottish villages has tended, in no small degree, to brutalise the game'"

**Significance**: Extreme prejudice against Scottish working-class players

**Shows**: Class and national tensions intersecting with professionalism debate

### Importation Hardened Attitudes

**Curry**: "Importation hardened attitudes and, quite often, strong opinions were exchanged"

**Pattern**: Scottish migration to England "in search of financial reward for playing football"

**Favoured Destination**: Lancashire in particular during the 1880s

**Curry's Question**: "Who was the first of their number [Scotsmen] to make the long trek south?"

**Article's Purpose**: "The remainder of this article will examine many of the ongoing historical processes taking place at this point in time"

**Implication**: James J. Lang may have been among the very first professional Scottish imports to English football

## Significance: Three Forms of Emergent Professionalism Documented

### Form 1: Billy Mosforth (Local Player Selling Services)

**Pattern**: Sheffield-based player selling skills to highest bidder within local area

**Evidence**: Shirt-changing incident (October 1884) - bribed mid-match with "ten bob and free drinks all week"

**Represented**: Multiple Sheffield clubs (Albion, Wednesday, Lockwood Brothers, Sheffield Rovers)

**Never**: Transferred to other geographical areas

### Form 2: Importation (Cursham, Scottish Players)

**Pattern**: Bringing players from other regions/countries specifically to strengthen teams

**Examples**:
- Arthur William Cursham (Nottingham to Sheffield, 1877-1878)
- Scottish players migrating to Lancashire (1880s)

**Tensions**:
- Limited chances for indigenous talent
- Unfair advantage in competitions
- National and class prejudices ("scum of the Scottish villages")

### Form 3: Multiple Club Representation (Both Mosforth and Cursham)

**Pattern**: Players appearing for different clubs in same season

**Legal**: In friendly matches vs cup ties distinction

**But**: "Much frowned upon in local soccer communities"

**Confused Situation**: "In relation to club loyalty"

### Early Life and Background

**Born**: March 1851

### The Industrial Accident (1869)

**Nickname**: "Reddie"

**The Secret**: "Said to have guarded the secret of his disability closely, perhaps not wishing to grant his opponents any advantage"

## Contact from Sheffield Wednesday (1876)

### Two Key Matches That Led to His Move

**Match 1**: Glasgow vs Sheffield (February 1876)
- Lang played for Glasgow
- Probably contacted by Wednesday officials, players, or both

**Match 2**: Clydesdale vs Sheffield Wednesday (April 1876)
- Lang played for Clydesdale
- 10,000 spectators attended
- "Invited to move to the city and play for the club"

**Significance**: These representative matches served as recruitment opportunities for Sheffield clubs

## First Season in Sheffield (1876-77)

### Confusion About First Match

**Sparling's Claim**: "Lang's 'first match for Wednesday was on the first Saturday of October at Myrtle Road…'"

**But**: No record of such fixture in Sheffield's two main newspapers of the time

**Only Recorded Game**: 7 October, eleven-a-side match at Attercliffe
- Lang's name not present in local paper's report

### First Extant Mention: 25 November 1876

**Match**: Sheffield Wednesday vs Hallam (friendly game)

**Significance**: "Prestigious match...in the locality," but "not a cup tie"

**Reporter's Discussion of Lang**:

> "It may not be out of place here to state that the Lang mentioned above is the celebrated player who has been one of the representatives of Glasgow v Sheffield. He has come to reside here, and will we believe assist the Wednesday team in their cup tie against Kimberworth on Saturday next"

**Key Phrase**: "Has come to **reside here**"

**Implication**: By no means Lang's first match for Wednesday - he had already played several games

---

## Lang's 1876-77 Season Statistics

### Residential Status

**Evidence Indicates**: "Lang was resident in Sheffield for virtually the whole of the football season"

### Matches Played for Wednesday

**Total Appearances**:
- **6 friendly matches**
- **4 cup ties**
- **2 representative fixtures**
- **1 benefit game**

**Cup Ties**: Present for every Sheffield Association cup tie

**Friendlies**: Not present in every friendly fixture

### Was Lang Employed for Whole Season or Just Cup Ties?

**Curry's Assessment**: "Seems fair to presume that Lang had been **'employed' for the whole season**, rather than having been brought down from Glasgow for cup ties alone"

**Evidence**: Of ten games, four were cup fixtures
- If only brought for cups, ratio would be different
- Played in friendlies, representative matches, benefit game

**Context**: "Fixtures in the 1870s were more intermittent than now"
- Amount of football Wednesday played "not unusual for the time"

**Financial Inducement**: "No indication of financial inducement"

## Monday Matches: Evidence of "Broken-Time" Payment

### The Monday Match Pattern

**1876-77 Season**: At least three of Lang's matches played on Monday

**Significance**: "May be indicative of a tendency towards reward for those participants requiring some form of early **'broken-time' payment**"

### Additional Monday Matches

**1879-80 Season**: One further Monday fixture

**1880-81 Season**: Three additional Monday games plus one Thursday match

**Pattern**: "Whilst this practice did not seem to occur every week, there is evidence to show that it was taking place on a **fairly regular basis**"

### Saint Monday

**Common Practice**: "Common practice among workers around this time of failing to register for work on Monday, effectively treating the day as leisure time"

**Known As**: "The day itself became known as **Saint Monday**"

**Implication**: Monday matches allowed working-class players to play without missing work (since they wouldn't work Monday anyway), but may also have involved payment

## Dispute with Sheffield Association Over Team Selection

### The Glasgow Selection Controversy

**Issue**: Lang involved in dispute with Sheffield Association over selection of team to face Glasgow

**Possible Reasons**:

1. **Genuine Grievance**: "May have been genuinely aggrieved concerning the failures or otherwise of the trial process"

2. **Convenient Excuse**: "May have been more than a little reticent in regard to facing his home association and found the disagreement a convenient excuse for not being involved"

## Peter Andrews: The Comparison Case

### Background

**Who Was He**: Another Scotsman who came south and played football in England

**Higher Status Than Lang**: "Much higher status than James J. Lang"

### Early Career and Move South

**February 1875**: Represented Glasgow against Sheffield
- "No doubt made acquaintances with players and representatives from the Yorkshire city"

**March 1875**: Capped by Scotland against England
- Scored in 2-2 draw

**Early 1876**: "Forced through his job to move to Leeds"

**Still Playing for Glasgow**: February 1876 (Glasgow vs Sheffield)

**April 1876**: Helped Clydesdale beat Sheffield Wednesday
- 10,000 spectators
- Same match where Lang also played
- "Allowed interested officials of Sheffield clubs to inquire as to the availability" of players

### Evidence of Agreement to Move

**At April 1876 Match**: "Evidence to suggest that at the latter game Lang agreed to come south to play for Wednesday and Andrews may also have made a similar agreement with Heeley"

## Who Arrived First: Lang or Andrews?

### The Newspaper Evidence

**Lang's Admission** (newspaper article published almost 25 years later):
- First match October 1876

**But Sheffield and Rotherham Independent** (3 November 1876):
- Specifically mentions Andrews playing for Heeley club against Thursday Wanderers in cup tie
- "Should we choose to believe the reporter...then it was **Andrews and not Lang who was the first Scotsman to play in the city**"

### Andrews' Move to Sheffield

**Initially From Leeds**: "Travelling the distance from Leeds to Sheffield to play for the Heeley club appears slightly prohibitive"

**Why Make the Journey**: "A Scottish international of Andrews' standard would have found South Yorkshire far more welcoming than the Leeds area in terms of footballing opportunities"

**Permanent Move**: "By at least the beginning of February 1877, Andrews had moved to Sheffield on a permanent basis"

**Probably Operating From Home**: Address may have been company headquarters, "more likely that Andrews was operating from home"

**Family**: Wife Anna and three children

**Children's Birthplaces**:
- Older two: Scotland

**Confirms**: Family moved to Sheffield permanently circa 1876-1877

**Implication**: "Makes it seem **less likely that Andrews received or indeed required money for playing football**"

### Glasgow Connections

**Clubs**: Eastern club (confirmed)

**Hint of Link**: Queen's Park

## Two Different Types of Migration

### Peter Andrews: Work-Related Move

**Curry's Assessment**: "Peter Andrews' move south was **almost certainly linked with his work**"

**Pattern**: Professional/middle-class Scotsman relocating for career, happens to also play football

### James J. Lang: Football-Related Move

**Curry's Assessment**: "James Lang's was probably **prompted partly by Wednesday's wish to strengthen their chances of winning a trophy**"

**Evidence**: One source clearly states:

> "J. J. Lang – a resident of Glasgow, from which place he journeyed to Sheffield **to play for the cup** [The Sheffield Football Association Challenge Cup]"

**Significance**: Move primarily motivated by football opportunity, not other employment

---

## The Sheffield Challenge Cup: Catalyst for Importation (1876-77)

### Introduction of New Competition

**Season**: 1876-77 heralded in local press "because of the introduction of this new cup competition"

**Local Press Report**:

> "The Challenge Cup contests are sure to create a great stir amongst the football players in the district. … There is evidently a great desire amongst the players to be selected in the contending sides, and many have been hard at work practising for a month. The prize, a £50 sterling silver cup, is to be contested for annually. … The eleven winners every year will have silver medals awarded to each of them"

**Prize Value**: £50 sterling silver cup (very substantial prize)

**Individual Medals**: Silver medals for each of eleven winners

**Impact**: Created strong incentive for clubs to strengthen teams

---

## Lang's Changed Role: From Resident to Importation (1877-78)

### Return to Scotland

**What Changed**: "At the beginning of the 1877-8 season...had returned to Scotland"

**New Club**: October 1877, began playing for 3rd Lanarkshire Rifle Volunteers (Third Lanark)

**Primary Location**: "For the majority of the 1877-8 season, he played football in the city of Glasgow"

### Importation for Cup Matches Only

**New Pattern**: "In the New Year, he would **travel down especially to assist Wednesday in local Challenge Cup matches**"

**Three Matches, All Cup Ties**:
1. **January 1878**: vs Hallam (cup match)
2. **February 1878**: vs Derby (cup match)
3. **March 1878**: vs Attercliffe in Final (cup match)
   - Wednesday won 2-0

**Only Appearances**: "These three games were Lang's only appearances for the club that season"

**Curry's Conclusion**: "Therefore, he seems to have been **specifically invited to play in competitive matches**"

### Radical Change in Employment Model

**Clear Indication**: "Data for the 1877-8 campaign clearly indicates that his services were engaged **solely for matches in the local cup competition**"

**Significance**: "As such, his role and that of his 'employers' had radically changed"

**Pattern**: From full-season resident player (1876-77) to imported specialist for cup matches only (1877-78)

## The 1878 Sheffield Challenge Cup Final vs Scotland International

### A Revealing Conflict

**Date**: 2 March 1878

**Two Events Same Day**:
1. Sheffield Challenge Cup Final (Lang played)

## Significance of Lang's Story for Understanding Professionalism

### Multiple Forms Demonstrated

1. **Full-Season Employment** (1876-77): Employed for whole season, cover job provided

2. **Imported Specialist** (1877-78, 1881-82): Brought in specifically for cup matches

3. **Exhibition Matches** (1879-80): Sheffield Zulus charging fees, levied from gate receipts

4. **Multiple Club Representation**: Played for Spital, Attercliffe, Providence while with Wednesday

5. **Broken-Time Payment**: Monday and Thursday matches

### Evolution of Employment Models

**From**: Full-season resident with cover job

**To**: Imported specialist for prestigious matches only

**Also**: Exhibition circuit with direct fee payments

**Significance**: Shows professionalism taking multiple experimental forms before 1885 legalization

### The Cover Job Revelation

**Critical Evidence**: Fearnehough job "merely appears to have been a cover"

**Reality**: "Time chiefly devoted to football and reading news"

**Implication**: De facto professional footballer with nominal other employment

**Pattern**: Would become common in later professional football (bogus jobs to maintain amateur status)

### Social Class Indicators

**Working Class Background**:
- Shipyard worker
- Officially blind/disabled
- Boilermaker (nominal)
- Later general dyer/cotton dyer
- Large family (15+ children)

**Contrast With**: Peter Andrews (lower middle class, insurance agent, didn't need football money)

**Significance**: Payment for playing football crucial for working-class players like Lang

### Geographic Mobility for Football

**Moved**:
- Glasgow to Sheffield (1876)
- Sheffield to Glasgow and back multiple times (1877-79)
- Sheffield to Manchester area (1885)

**Traveled For Matches**:
- Chesterfield (Spital club)
- Throughout North, Midlands, Scotland (Zulus)
- Possibly Northwich Victoria

**Significance**: Professional football creating new patterns of labor migration
