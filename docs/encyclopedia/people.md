# Key Figures in Sheffield Rules Football

A comprehensive directory of the individuals who shaped Sheffield Rules football and its relationship with the Football Association.

---

## Sheffield Figures

### Harry Walker Chambers

**Role**: Sheffield Football Club member, former secretary, Sheffield FA President (1867-1869)

**Background**:
- Lawyer by profession
- Educated in London
- Studying for law exams in London during FA formation (1863)
- Father's practice in Sheffield used London agents Pitman and Lane (near King's Walk and Morley)

**Key Contributions**:

**October 1863**:
- Possibly present at gatherings leading to the FA's formation
- May have mentioned to Ebenezer Cobb Morley that Sheffield clubs played to a standard code
- This may have catalyzed the idea of creating a unified football association

**2 January 1864**:
- Selected to play in FA exhibition game at London
- Played for Arthur Pember's President's side vs. Morley's Secretary's side
- First game under FA rules with club representatives
- Dined at Grosvenor Hotel, Pimlico afterwards
- Likely realized more needed to be done in Sheffield to promote FA cause

**31 March 1866** - Battersea Park Match:
- Part of Sheffield team (actually Sheffield FC only)
- Match noted for physical and robust style of play
- Attended Albion Hotel dinner afterward with London officials
- Sat at top table with Chesterman, Pember, Morley, possibly Kinnaird

**26 February 1867** - FA AGM:
- Letter read from Chambers as President of Sheffield Football Association
- Stated association represented 14 clubs and 1000-1200 members

**6 March 1867** - Sheffield FA Formation:
- Chairman at founding meeting at Adelphi Hotel
- Presided over election of democratic committee structure

**Leadership**:
- First President of Sheffield Football Association (1867-1869)
- Did not stay long as President
- By 1869, replaced by John Charles Shaw
- Moved out of football leadership

**December 1871**:
- Served as London's umpire for return match at Bramall Lane
- Despite being from Sheffield, officiated for the visiting London team
- Shows his continued connection to both Sheffield and London football

**Legacy**:
- Critical bridge between Sheffield and London football
- London connections and legal background gave him access to FA leadership
- Instrumental in organizing Youdan Cup and forming Sheffield FA
- With Chambers and Chesterman out of equation by early 1870s, Shaw and Alcock became driving forces

### William Chesterman

**Role**: Sheffield Football Club Secretary, FA Committee Member

**Background**:
- Secretary of Sheffield Football Club
- Represented Sheffield FC in dealings with London FA

**Key Contributions**:

**November 1863**:
- Wrote to complain about hacking in draft FA rules
- Clear that vicious practice had nothing to do with football

**22 February 1866** - Letter to FA:
- Couldn't send delegate but had no objection to proposed alterations
- Glad the fair catch clause would be expunged
- Stated rules were nearly the same as FA rules
- Both decidedly against hacking, throttling, or wrestling
- **Suggested advisability of Sheffield playing a picked London team**

**12 February 1867** (per Percy Young):
- Travelled to London to amend certain rules to fit Sheffield style of play
- Unsuccessful
- Reception recorded in FA minute book

**26 February 1867** - FA AGM:
- Present at meeting
- Made statement accepting FA superiority:
  > "The alterations the Sheffield men proposed were not of much consequence, as they would be guided by the results of that night's deliberations, giving all their attention to the superior alterations that the London representatives might make."
- FA rejected Sheffield amendments
- Chesterman and Chambers had no option but to accept 1867 FA rules

**Youdan Cup Era**:
- Along with Chambers, organized committee for competition
- Ensured agreement and framework for tournament
- Desperate to force issue of FA rule adoption

**FA Committee Service**:
- Remained on FA committee after Sheffield FA formation
- Represented out-of-London interests

**October 1871**:
- Failed to be re-elected to London FA committee
- Lost his seat as out-of-London representative

**1872**:
- Replaced by Walter Skinner (viewed as Shaw's man)

**Legacy**:
- With Chambers and Chesterman out of equation, Shaw and Alcock became driving forces
- Represented generation of Sheffield leadership that subordinated itself to London
- His diplomatic approach to FA may have been seen as too deferential by Sheffield clubs

### John Charles Shaw

**Role**: Captain of Hallam Club, Sheffield FA President (1869-1883)

**Background**:
- From Penistone and district (15 miles north of Sheffield)
- According to Curry, area played significant part in development of modern club football in Sheffield
- Captain of Hallam Club during Youdan Cup era

**Key Contributions**:

**31 March 1866** - Battersea Park:
- Part of Sheffield team (though still captain of Hallam Club at time)
- Possibly sat with Charles Alcock during Albion Hotel dinner
- Both had much in common: established own clubs, would achieve authority in associations

**28 January 1867**:
- Member of Youdan Cup organizing committee
- Represented Hallam Club interests

**5 March 1867**:
- Captain of Hallam Club when they won Youdan Cup
- Defeated Norfolk 0 goals 2 rouges to 0 goals 0 rouges at Bramall Lane
- Final attracted crowd over 3000

**Trophy Presentation**:
- Presented silver claret jug to Hallam club (as their captain)
- After closing match of season using new Sheffield FA rules
- Trophy still in possession of Hallam club

**1869**:
- Became President of Sheffield Football Association
- Replaced Harry Walker Chambers

**Presidency (1869-1883)**:
- Held "chair" for fourteen years
- Oversaw rule changes
- Oversaw eventual amalgamation with London in 1877
- Most significant Sheffield football administrator of the era

**Walter Skinner Connection**:
- Skinner (viewed as Shaw's man) replaced Chesterman on FA committee in 1872
- By time Shaw became Sheffield FA President, Skinner was already honorary secretary
- Shaw had his own representative in London

**December 1871** - Return Match:
- Sheffield FA President when London visited Bramall Lane
- London side played Sheffield rules
- London short one man - Shaw stepped in to make up numbers for London
- Shows diplomatic nature and sportsmanship
- Dined at Adelphi Hotel with Charles Alcock (visiting captain)
- Alcock undoubtedly sat next to Shaw
- Alcock possibly sought last-minute reassurances regarding framework of Youdan Cup's success
- Regarding his forthcoming FA Challenge Cup competition

**New Power Dynamic**:
- With Chambers and Chesterman out of equation
- Shaw and Alcock became driving forces of respective organizations
- Unlike predecessors, Shaw maintained Sheffield's independence while cooperating with London

**Legacy**:
- Longest-serving Sheffield FA President of the founding era
- Successfully navigated Sheffield-London relations
- Oversaw critical period including amalgamation in 1877
- Geographic connection to Penistone important for club football development

### John Tomlinson

**Role**: Leading Broomhall Club Official, Referee

**Key Contributions**:

**Youdan Cup 1867**:
- Leading official at Broomhall club
- Appointed to referee semi-final (2 March 1867)
- Appointed to referee final (5 March 1867)
- Part of neutral referee system introduced to avoid disputes
- Referee's decision was final - unique feature for football at the time

**Significance**:
- FA adopted this strategy (neutral referees for latter stages) for Challenge Cup in 1871
- Demonstrated Sheffield's organizational innovation
- Precedent-setting role in football officiating

**Legacy**:
- Part of committee's thoughtful tournament structure
- Helped establish neutral officiating as standard practice
- His work in Youdan Cup influenced future cup competition organization

### John Marsh

**Role**: Captain of Wednesday FC, Cromwell Cup Winner

**Background**:
- From Penistone and district (15 miles north of Sheffield)
- Geographic connection: same area as John Charles Shaw
- According to Curry, Penistone area played significant part in development of modern club football in Sheffield

**Key Contributions**:

**Cromwell Cup 1868**:
- Captain of Wednesday FC (first season club)
- Led team through competition

**1 February 1868** - First Round:
- Wednesday defeated Exchange 4 goals 0 rouge to 0 goals 0 rouges
- Venue: Mackenzie

**15 February 1868** - Final:
- Wednesday defeated Garrick 1 goal 0 rouges to 0 goals 0 rouges
- Venue: Bramall Lane
- Attendance: 600

**16 March 1868** - Trophy Presentation:
- Trophy presented to Marsh as captain
- Entire team presented on stage of Theatre Royal
- Part of benefit evening for Oliver Cromwell (leaving the area)

**Legacy**:
- Cromwell Cup still in possession of Sheffield Wednesday Football Club
- Geographic link with Shaw from Penistone shows regional importance in Sheffield football development
- Led Wednesday in their first season to cup victory

### Walter Skinner

**Role**: Sheffield FA Honorary Secretary, FA Committee Member

**Key Contributions**:

**1872**:
- Replaced William Chesterman on FA committee
- Viewed as "John Charles Shaw's man"
- By time Shaw became Sheffield FA President, Skinner was already honorary secretary

**Significance**:
- Represented new generation of Sheffield-London relations
- Unlike Chesterman's deferential approach, Skinner worked as Shaw's representative
- Maintained Sheffield's interests in London while cooperating with FA
- Part of Shaw's successful strategy for navigating Sheffield-London relations

**Legacy**:
- Part of power dynamic shift: Chambers and Chesterman out, Shaw and Alcock as driving forces
- Helped Sheffield maintain stronger position in negotiations with London
- Represented more assertive Sheffield approach to FA relations

## London (FA) Figures

### Arthur Pember

**Role**: FA President, Lloyd's worker

**Background**:
- Worked at Lloyd's (not a solicitor)
- Early misreporting claimed FA inaugural meeting at his chambers
- Actually held at Ebenezer Cobb Morley's chambers

**Key Contributions**:

**October 1863**:
- Involved in gatherings leading to FA formation
- Senior figure in establishing the association

**2 January 1864** - Exhibition Match:
- Captained President's side
- Harry Chambers played on his team vs. Morley's Secretary's side
- First game under FA rules with club representatives
- Hosted dinner at Grosvenor Hotel, Pimlico

**31 March 1866** - Battersea Park Match:
- FA President during first representative match
- Played in London team against Sheffield
- London won convincingly, Sheffield failed to score
- Attended Albion Hotel dinner afterward
- Sat at top table with Morley, Chambers, Chesterman, possibly Kinnaird

**Albion Hotel Dinner**:
- Likely led speeches and toasts centered on football
- Topic: unification of clubs under same rules
- May have pointed out to Chambers and Chesterman that other Sheffield teams hadn't experienced London rules
- Other Sheffield teams didn't have a voice

**Legacy**:
- Part of FA leadership that established London's ascendency
- Represented elite social group that Sheffield FC sought to emulate
- Presided over FA during critical period of Sheffield-London relations

### Ebenezer Cobb Morley

**Role**: FA Secretary, Solicitor at King's Walk

**Background**:
- Solicitor with chambers at King's Walk, London
- Near Pitman and Lane (agents for Chambers' father's practice)
- Key figure in FA formation

**Key Contributions**:

**October 1863**:
- Central figure in gatherings leading to FA formation
- Inaugural meeting held at his chambers (not Pember's)
- Harry Chambers possibly mentioned to him that Sheffield clubs played to standard code

**November 1863**:
- Draft FA rules included hacking
- William Chesterman wrote to complain

**December 1863**:
- FA rules refined: hacking and running with ball removed
- New laws still contained elements contrary to Sheffield game

**2 January 1864** - Exhibition Match:
- Captained Secretary's side vs. Pember's President's side
- Harry Chambers played on opposing team
- Hosted dinner at Grosvenor Hotel, Pimlico

**22 February 1866** - FA AGM:
- Read Chesterman's letter proposing match between Sheffield and London
- Facilitated FA rule changes including touch downs, modified offside, throw-in rules

**31 March 1866** - Battersea Park Match:
- FA Secretary during first representative match
- Played in London team
- Attended Albion Hotel dinner at top table

**26 February 1867** - FA AGM:
- Numbers dwindled to six clubs
- Considered disbanding
- Letter from Chambers (read by Robert Willis) had impact: 14 clubs, 1000-1200 members
- Sheffield letter revitalized FA fortunes

**Legacy**:
- Critical figure in FA survival
- Sheffield's organization and numbers may have saved FA from disbanding in 1867
- Represented London's initially fragile position before FA grew strong

### Charles William Alcock

**Role**: Player, Journalist (*The Sportsman*), Future FA Authority, FA Cup Creator

**Background**:
- Player for London clubs
- Journalist for *The Sportsman* newspaper
- Future international player

**Key Contributions**:

**31 March 1866** - Battersea Park Match:
- Played in London team against Sheffield
- Future international
- Possibly sat with John Charles Shaw during Albion Hotel dinner
- Both had much in common: established own clubs, would achieve authority in their associations

**On Sheffield Game**:
- Described Sheffield game as "loose and disjointed"
- Observed it directly encouraged passing with players dispersed over field
- Contrasted with FA rules' physicality and attrition

**14 March 1867** - *The Sportsman* Report:
- Journalist when paper ran report entitled "Sheffield Football Association"
- Claimed meeting took place 6 March at Adelphi Hotel to discuss rules
- Coverage helped document Sheffield FA formation

**October 1871**:
- Launched FA Challenge Cup
- Matthew Taylor: "Would be surprising if Charles Alcock had not been inspired by the apparent success of the Youdan competition"

**December 1871** - Return Match:
- Brought representative London side to play Sheffield at Bramall Lane
- **London side played Sheffield rules** (controversial decision)
- London short one man - Shaw stepped in to make up numbers for London
- Played against wishes of London committee
- Knew full well they would be playing different code

**Post-Match Dinner at Adelphi Hotel**:
- Visiting team captain
- Undoubtedly sat next to John Charles Shaw (Sheffield FA President)
- Like Battersea game, what happened afterward was significant
- Possibly sought last-minute reassurances regarding framework of Youdan Cup's success
- Regarding his forthcoming FA Challenge Cup competition
- FA Cup had just launched in October 1871, this game in December

**New Power Dynamic**:
- With Chambers and Chesterman out of equation
- Shaw and Alcock became driving forces of respective organizations
- Both represented new generation of leadership
- More collaborative than previous Sheffield-London relations

**Legacy**:
- FA Cup creator, directly influenced by Youdan Cup
- Maintained relationship with Sheffield football
- Willing to play under Sheffield rules (unique among London leadership)
- Documented Sheffield football developments through journalism
- "The Rest is History" - his FA Cup became world's most famous football competition

## Sponsors

### Thomas Youdan

**Role**: Owner of Alexandra Music Hall on Blonk Street, Philanthropist

**Background**:
- Owned Alexandra Music Hall on Blonk Street, Sheffield
- Well known to town's footballers - favourite port of call
- Regarded as a maverick
- Used every marketing ploy to entice public to his establishment
- Known for philanthropy in promoting local causes
- Donated monies for cups and prizes

**Youdan Cup Sponsorship**:

**Role**:
- **Did not create tournament structure** - merely provided sponsorship
- Likely approached by committee member (possibly Chambers or Chesterman) with idea
- Agreed to sponsor silver cup for competition

**Benefits for Youdan**:
- Linked with popular tournament
- Welcome revenue driver for his establishment
- **Early example of commercial sponsorship in football**
- Marketing opportunity to promote Alexandra Music Hall

**Trophy**:
- Commissioned trophy design competition (January 1867)
- Cash prize offered for best design
- Winning design not completed in time
- Silver claret jug bought "off the shelf" as replacement
- Trophy now valued at over one million pounds

**The Theft - 1 March 1867**:
- Vast sum of £70 stolen from Youdan's offices (£8000 by 2023 standards)
- Westby claims this money was raised during tournament
- **Before** gate monies taken for final (5 March) and second place game (9 March)
- Money may have had no link with football
- Mystery remains about what the money was for

**Player Welfare Support**:
- Assisted with benefit society fundraising in subsequent season
- Evenings at Alexandra Music Hall dedicated to the fund
- Supported Town vs. Country football match staged at Bramall Lane

**Why Only One Year?**:
- Youdan Cup not held again after 1867
- Most likely because purpose had been served (formation of Sheffield FA)
- Collins (podcast): Youdan lost interest due to difficulty obtaining drinks licence
  - From William Clegg (aggressive teetotaller)
  - Father of John Charles Clegg (future FA President)
- Rivalry with Oliver Cromwell may have also been factor

**Legacy**:
- Pioneer of commercial sponsorship in football
- Youdan Cup inspired FA Challenge Cup (per Matthew Taylor)
- Trophy remains with Hallam Club as historical artifact
- Name forever associated with world's first adult football knockout competition

### Oliver Cromwell

**Role**: Stage manager of Theatre Royal

**Background**:
- Stage manager of Theatre Royal in Sheffield
- Direct competition with Thomas Youdan's Alexandria Music Hall
- Theatrical rivalry extended to football sponsorship

**Cromwell Cup Sponsorship (1868)**:

**Format**:
- Minor knockout competition
- For organizations not exceeding 2 years of age
- Four clubs participated
- Played under existing Sheffield rules (not FA rules)
- Teams played 14-a-side (not 12-a-side like Youdan Cup)

**Participating Clubs**:
1. Garrick (two seasons old)
2. Wellington (two seasons old)
3. Wednesday (first season) - Winners
4. Exchange (first season)

**Final - 15 February 1868**:
- Venue: Bramall Lane
- Attendance: 600
- Wednesday defeated Garrick 1 goal 0 rouges to 0 goals 0 rouges

**Trophy Presentation - 16 March 1868**:
- Presented to John Marsh (captain of Wednesday)
- Entire team presented on stage of Theatre Royal
- Part of benefit evening for Cromwell (leaving the area)
- Used his own venue for maximum promotional impact

**Why Only One Year?**:
- Cromwell was leaving the area
- Benefit evening was farewell event
- No one continued sponsorship after his departure

**Legacy**:
- Cromwell Cup still in possession of Sheffield Wednesday Football Club
- Like Youdan Cup, only held once
- Demonstrated competitive commercial sponsorship in early football
- Theatre Royal vs. Alexandra Music Hall rivalry played out through football cups
