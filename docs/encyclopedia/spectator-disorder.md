# Spectator Disorder in Victorian Sheffield Football (1881-1892)

## Overview

Research into football crowd behaviour in Victorian Sheffield through examination of four major incidents of spectator misbehaviour. While disorder appears relatively infrequent, specific incidents reveal patterns of working-class frustration, class tensions, and challenges to emerging football professionalism.

**Research by**: Graham Curry

**Published in**: "Football Spectatorship in mid-to-late Victorian Sheffield", *Soccer & Society*, 2007

**Research Period**: 1881-1892

**Primary Sources**: Sheffield newspapers (exhaustive trawl)

---

## Research Methodology and Context

### Initial Research Goals

**Original Intention**: Research football fan culture in Victorian Sheffield
- Crowd composition
- Attendances
- General spectator behaviour

**Metamorphosis**: Concentrated on crowd disorder instead

**Why**: Using local newspapers as primary sources means:
- Researcher mostly discovers bad behaviour rather than good
- Reporting influenced by what reading public thought newsworthy
- Bad news sells papers

**Result**: Examination of one element of spectator sub-culture through close examination of four particular examples

### Overall Finding

**After exhaustive trawl** (Sheffield newspapers 1881-1892):
> "It is difficult to argue that football spectator misbehaviour was a major problem in the city during the period studied"

**However**:
- This is not to say it did not exist
- Four major incidents indicate misbehaviour occasionally present
- Worrying enough for sporting press to draw attention and pass opinion

**Pattern**: Three of four incidents occurred **October 1890 - January 1892** (sixteen months)
- All at Sheffield Wednesday home fixtures
- All at Olive Grove ground (just south of city centre)
- Shows concentration in time and place

## The Four Major Incidents

### 1. The Pierce Dix Incident - 24 January 1881

**Context**: Local Enmities Surface during Crowd Misbehaviour

#### Background: The Sheffield Zulus Controversy

**1879-1881**: Tales of professionalism surfaced
- Previously mentioned: Sheffield Zulus team
- Played dressed as members of Southern African tribe
- Practice cut short by local association
- Number of players banned
- Quickly reinstated following appropriate apologies

**January 1881**: Another Zulu game discovered
- **11 players suspended**
- Lengthy correspondence began in Sheffield newspapers

#### Jack Hunter's Omission

**The Issue**:
- North vs South representative match at Bramall Lane
- Jack Hunter (noteworthy player) omitted from team
- Many contributors expressed surprise

**William Pierce Dix's Response**:
- Honorary Secretary of Sheffield FA
- Stated Hunter's Zulu activities reason for non-selection

**Pierce Dix's Importance**:
- Important character in English football at time
- Honorary Secretary of Sheffield FA
- Important referee
- Respected nationally and locally as football administrator

#### The Wharncliffe Cup Semi-Final

**Impact of Bans**:
- Deprived Heeley and Wednesday of important team members
- For semi-final of Wharncliffe Cup (local competition)

**The Match**:
- **Pierce Dix chosen to referee** this sensitive fixture
- Wednesday emerged comfortable victors: 7-2
- Wharncliffe Cup competition for 1880-81 ultimately abandoned

#### The Assault on Pierce Dix

**Sheffield and Rotherham Independent Reporter**:
> "At the Wharncliffe Charity Cup match between Heeley and Wednesday on Monday last, there was quite a scene and remarks, of a character not calculated to enhance football in the minds of the gentler sex, were loud and frequent, Mr Dix being the object of the wrath of the multitude. After the match he was **grossly assaulted by those who should certainly have set a better example to the lower order** and the whole affair had such an effect on the official named that he almost immediately tendered his resignation."

**Critical Details**:
- "Grossly assaulted"
- Assailants described as those "who should have set better example to lower order"
- **Implies assailants were from higher social class**
- Not working-class "mob" but respectable individuals
- Dix almost immediately tendered resignation

#### Charles Clegg's Response

**Defence of Brother-in-Law**:
- Charles Clegg penned letter in defence of Pierce Dix
- Already noted as influential figure in English football
- **Vociferous critic of professionalism**

**Sheffield FA Meeting**:
- Delegates expressed "their disgust at the personal attack upon Mr Dix"
- Voiced support for his subsequent actions

**Resolution**:
- Zulu players apologised
- Reinstated February 1881

#### Jack Hunter's Reinstatement

**12 February 1881**:
- Hunter represented Sheffield against Glasgow
- Named as captain for occasion

**Context of Trial Match**:
- Hunter's Zulu activities would have forced him to miss North vs South international trial
- Trial scheduled Saturday 22 January in Sheffield
- **Postponed due to bad weather**
- Following apology, Hunter reinstated
- Played in re-arranged fixture Saturday 19 February

**International Career**:
- Subsequently picked for two of England's internationals
- vs Scotland
- vs Wales (appointed captain)
- Shows quick rehabilitation

#### Significance

**Class Dynamics**:
- Assailants from higher social class ("should have set better example")
- Not typical working-class hooliganism
- Suggests middle-class involvement in crowd disorder

**Professionalism Tensions**:
- Zulus represented early professionalism
- Charles Clegg (vociferous anti-professionalism) defended Dix
- Bans punished professionalism
- Crowd anger at bans
- Shows contested nature of professionalism debate

### 2. Sheffield Wednesday v Crewe Alexandra - 18 October 1890

**Competition**: Football Alliance league

**Context**: Wednesday's Lean Spell
- Turned professional for 1887-88 season
- Rejected for entry into first Football League (1888-89)
- 12 unsuccessful applicants formed Football Alliance (1889-90)
- Wednesday **won Alliance in 1889-90**
- **Failed miserably to defend crown**
- **Finishing bottom of table in 1890-91**
- Two of three disturbances during lean spell
- May have been contributory factor in crowd's frustration

#### Football Officiating in 1890

**System**:
- **Referee**: Neutral official
- **Two Umpires**: Provided by each competing club
- In 2005 referred to as assistant referees (formerly linesmen)

**Expected Behavior**:
- Umpires appeal to referee's judgement if dispute occurred
- If both umpires appealed concerning same dispute, referee made decision
- Marked transition from player self-management to external officiating

#### William Robert Wake - The Volunteer Umpire

**The Situation**:
- Wednesday lacking umpire at start of match
- Call made for volunteer from crowd
- **William Robert Wake stepped up**
- Took place as Wednesday umpire
- **Despite objections from Sheffield players**

**Wake's Background**:
- Local solicitor
- Ex-player for Thursday Wanderers club
- Former Yorkshire cricketer
- **Had been involved in disagreement with certain Wednesday players in previous season**
- May have wished to exact revenge

**During the Game**:
- Wake seemingly failed to make certain expected appeals for Wednesday
- Felt responsible for Crewe goal which spectators and players thought offside
- Wednesday lost the match

#### The Disturbance

**At Conclusion of Game**:
> "A number of spectators, probably some 700 or 800, excitedly swarmed round the officials as they left the ground uttering cries of anger and disappointment"

**Crowd Size Analysis**:
- Total attendance: around **7,000**
- Involved in disturbance: **700-800**
- **10-11% of crowd involved**
- "Quite a high percentage"
- "Hard to argue this represented small percentage"

**Wake's Confrontations**:

1. **First Incident**:
   - Wake stood in front of committee room under main stand
   - Entered argument with **"a tall and respectably dressed individual"**
   - Both ushered inside by policemen

2. **Second Incident**:
   - Wake **re-emerged** from committee room
   - Began arguing with remaining crowd
   - Still hundreds in front of committee rooms
   - Eventually persuaded to leave ground by policemen

**Class Observation**:
- One crowd member described as **"respectably dressed"**
- Individual from same relatively high social class as Pierce Dix's assailants (1881)
- **Not just working-class disorder**

#### Significance

**Revenge Motive**: Wake's previous disagreement with Wednesday players
- Personal vendetta
- Strange representation because of probable bad blood
- Not typical neutral officiating

**Press Reaction**:
- Worthy of good deal of adverse press coverage
- Precursor of things to come on Sheffield football scene

**Threatening Behavior**:
- Crowd towards official
- Though strange circumstances
- Shocked elements of press into effusive comment

### 3. Sheffield Wednesday v Newton Heath - 24 January 1891

**Competition**: Football Alliance

**Result**: Another defeat for Wednesday (continuing poor form)

#### The Incident

**Sheffield and Rotherham Independent**:
> "A very unseemly display of hostility was made towards the referee by a portion of the spectators"

**The Decision**:
- Crowd deemed defeat directly caused by specific decision against home side
- Referee: **Mr L.G. Wright of Derby**

**The Escape**:
- Official had to be ushered off field by Wednesday committee members
- **Whole group pelted with mud** as they made escape

**The Wait**:
> "Some scores of excited and vengeance-threatening individuals of a somewhat rough appearance … remained in front of the stand waiting for the reappearance of the referee"

**Class Description**:
- "Rough appearance"
- Contrasts with "respectably dressed" in previous incidents
- Working-class involvement

#### Sheffield's Self-Image

**Tuesday Edition of Local Newspaper** (critical observation):
> "It has long been the proud boast of Sheffield footballers that spectators in the cutlery metropolis know how to behave themselves. They were not like those unmanerly [sic] Lancashire crowds at Darwen or Newton Heath, not they!"

**Two Important Implications**:

1. **Sheffield crowd behaviour perceived by reporters to be generally good**
   - Pride in local spectator culture
   - Self-image of superiority

2. **Compared to Lancashire football spectators, especially well behaved**
   - Specific mention of Darwen and Newton Heath
   - Lancashire seen as rough
   - Sheffield seen as respectable

**Caveat**: Local reporter may have been exhibiting considerable bias
- Regarding behaviour within city's footballing community
- Should be wary of comments regarding Sheffield life
- Home town bias

#### Newton Heath Bad Blood

**Evidence of Ill Feeling** between clubs:

**Previous Season (1889-90)**:
- Wednesday concluded programme at Newton Heath
- Season when Wednesday:
  - Won Football Alliance title
  - Reached FA Cup Final
  - Heavily defeated by Blackburn Rovers in final

**The Match** (at Newton Heath):
- Crowd: around 4,000
- Newton Heath had man sent off
- **Wednesday players attacked by home supporters**:
  - At half time
  - At full time
  - Two players injured

**Specific Assaults**:
1. **Billy Ingram**: Pushed over advertising board
2. **Teddy Brayshaw**: Assaulted with stick
3. **Harry Winterbottom** (injured captain, acting as umpire):
   - Had cap stolen
   - Felt fortunate to escape chasing mob

**Context**: Return fixture at Sheffield
- Previous violence fresh in memory
- May have contributed to hostile atmosphere
- Crowd seeking revenge for treatment of players?

### 4. Sheffield Wednesday v Small Heath - 30 January 1892

**Competition**: FA Cup Second Round

**Opponents**: Small Heath from Birmingham

**Result**: Wednesday won 2-0

**Context**: Final example of spectator misbehaviour at Wednesday home encounter

#### The Match

**Particularly Physical Affair**:
- **Two Sheffield players sent off**:
  1. Richardson
  2. Duncan Gemmell
- Shows rough game
- Crowd anger at sendings off

#### The Disturbance

**At Final Whistle**:
- Scenes reminiscent of two previous incidents
- **Portion of crowd rushed onto pitch**
- Headed for referee
- **Mud thrown** again
- Official surrounded and shepherded from field
  - By Wednesday officials
  - By police

#### Press Coverage

**Later Edition of Local Newspaper**:
- Reporter **attempted to play down misbehaviour**
- Keen to point out crowd's actions similar to:
  > "Those frequently seen at political and electioneering meetings"
- Normalizing the behaviour
- Comparing to accepted political rowdiness

**Description of Crowd**:
- People involved described as appearing **"not too refined"**
- Working-class involvement again
- Contrast with some "respectably dressed" in earlier incidents

#### The Correspondence

**Responses in Newspaper Correspondence Columns**:
- Several responses to happenings at Olive Grove
- None more interesting than **"An Old Footballer"**

**"An Old Footballer's" Letter**:
- Former spectator at Wednesday's games
- Felt **unable to attend at present time**
- Reason: **"The filthy language used on almost every occasion"**

**Significance**:
> "It seems clear that this type of crowd misbehaviour had continued for some time, though one cannot prove with any certainty the identities of the people responsible for the 'filthy language'"

**Implications**:
- Not isolated incidents
- Pattern of poor behaviour
- Verbal abuse alongside physical
- Long-standing problem
- Respectable fans being driven away

## Analysis and Patterns

### Temporal Pattern

**Concentration in Time**:
- 3 of 4 incidents: October 1890 - January 1892 (16 months)
- All 3 at Sheffield Wednesday's Olive Grove ground
- **Pierce Dix incident (1881)**: 9 years earlier, different context

**Wednesday's Context**:
- Poor form in 1890-91 season (bottom of table)
- Defending Alliance champions failing
- Crowd frustration at defeats
- Refusal to accept unpopular refereeing decisions

### Geographic Pattern

**All Wednesday Incidents at Olive Grove**:
- South of city centre
- Specific ground, specific club
- Not city-wide problem
- Localized to one venue

### Class Dynamics

**Mixed Class Involvement**:

1. **Middle/Upper Class**:
   - Pierce Dix assault: "those who should have set better example to lower order"
   - Wake incident: "tall and respectably dressed individual"
   - Not just working-class phenomenon

2. **Working Class**:
   - Newton Heath incident: "rough appearance"
   - Small Heath incident: "not too refined"
   - "Filthy language"
   - Traditional image of football hooliganism

**Significance**: Cross-class disorder
- Challenges assumption only working-class misbehaved
- Middle-class also capable of violence
- Shared frustration transcending class

### Types of Misbehaviour

**Physical Violence**:
- "Grossly assaulted" (Pierce Dix)
- Pelted with mud (Wright, Small Heath referee)
- Swarming officials (all incidents)
- Threatening behaviour

**Verbal Abuse**:
- "Remarks not calculated to enhance football in minds of gentler sex"
- "Cries of anger and disappointment"
- "Filthy language used on almost every occasion"

**Intimidation**:
- Waiting for referee to reappear
- "Vengeance-threatening individuals"
- Hundreds remaining after match
- Creating atmosphere of fear

### Targets

**Officials Primary Target**:
- Referee in 3 of 4 incidents
- Umpire in 1 incident (Wake)
- Honorary Secretary (Pierce Dix) in 1 incident

**Never Players**:
- Despite sendings off
- Despite poor performances
- Officials seen as cause of defeat

### Scale

**Crowd Sizes**:
- 7,000 total attendance (Crewe match)
- 700-800 involved in disorder (10-11%)
- "Some scores" waiting for referee (Newton Heath)
- "Portion of crowd" rushing pitch (Small Heath)

**Not Majority**: But significant minority
- Enough to create serious incidents
- Enough to require police intervention
- Enough to shock press

### Professionalism Context

**Pierce Dix Incident**:
- Directly related to professionalism (Zulus)
- Charles Clegg (anti-professional) involved
- Shows professionalism debate inflaming tensions

**Wednesday Incidents**:
- Club had turned professional (1887-88)
- Professional players sent off (Small Heath)
- Expectations higher with professionals
- Crowd demanding success for their money?

## Sheffield vs Lancashire

### Sheffield's Self-Image

**The Claim**:
> "It has long been the proud boast of Sheffield footballers that spectators in the cutlery metropolis know how to behave themselves"

**Comparison**:
> "They were not like those unmanerly Lancashire crowds at Darwen or Newton Heath"

**Reality Check**:
- Three major incidents in 16 months
- Mud throwing at referees
- Physical assaults
- Filthy language
- **Pride before fall**

### Newton Heath Violence (1890)

**At Newton Heath** (Wednesday players attacked):
- Billy Ingram pushed over advertising board
- Teddy Brayshaw assaulted with stick
- Harry Winterbottom's cap stolen, chased by mob

**Compared to Sheffield**:
- Newton Heath: Direct physical assaults on players
- Sheffield: Primarily targeting officials
- Both involve mud, physical violence, intimidation
- Question: Was Sheffield really better behaved?

**Reporter Bias**: Local newspapers defending Sheffield reputation
- May have downplayed incidents
- Compared favorably to Lancashire
- Protecting city's image

**Implies**:
- Problem existed for some time

### Official Responses

**Pierce Dix**:
- Tendered resignation (though presumably stayed)

**Sheffield FA**:
- Expressed disgust at attack on Dix
- Supported him
- Shows institutional concern

**Police Involvement**:
- Present at all three Wednesday incidents
- Had to shepherd officials
- Shows recognition of danger

## Conclusion

### Main Findings

1. **Spectator misbehaviour not major problem** in Sheffield 1881-1892
   - But did exist
   - Four major incidents documented
   - May be underreported

2. **Pattern at Sheffield Wednesday** (Oct 1890 - Jan 1892)
   - Three incidents in 16 months
   - All at Olive Grove ground
   - During poor form/bottom of table
   - Crowd frustration at defeats and refereeing

3. **Cross-class involvement**
   - Not just working-class hooliganism
   - "Respectably dressed" and "rough appearance"
   - Shared frustration transcending class

4. **Officials primary targets**
   - Referees, umpires, administrators
   - Physical violence, mud-throwing, intimidation
   - Shows challenge to emerging professional officiating

5. **Professionalism context**
   - Pierce Dix incident directly related
   - Wednesday incidents after professionalization
   - Changing expectations and frustrations

6. **Sheffield's self-image challenged**
   - Pride in good behaviour
   - Comparison to "unmanerly Lancashire crowds"
   - Reality: significant disorder existed
   - Reporter bias protecting reputation

### For Game Development

**Shows Victorian Football Not Idyllic**:
- Disorder alongside development
- Professionalism bringing new tensions
- Crowds demanding success
- Officials under pressure

**Patterns Recognizable Today**:
- Targeting referees
- Missile throwing (mud)
- Verbal abuse
- Minority spoiling for majority
- Respectable fans driven away
