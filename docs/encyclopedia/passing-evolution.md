# The Evolution of Football Passing in Nineteenth-Century Britain

## Overview

Passing is the most fundamental and frequent activity in modern association football, yet its historical development has received little detailed research. This entry examines how passing evolved from the "dribbling game" of the 1850s-1860s to the combination play that characterizes modern football, with particular focus on Sheffield's pioneering role.

**Key Finding**: Contemporary match reports demonstrate passing and combination play in Sheffield football as early as 1865, possibly 1861 - **predating** the commonly cited 1872 Scotland-England international by 7-11 years.

---

## The Myth of Scottish Origins

### Popular Narrative

**Common Claims in Literature and Media**: Passing was pioneered in Scotland

#### Wilson's Assertion

**Jonathan Wilson** (*Inverting the Pyramid*):
> "The spread of passing itself – that 'united action' – can be traced back to one game, football's first international, played between England and Scotland at Partick, the West of Scotland cricket ground, in 1872"

#### Kitching's Claim

**Gavin Kitching**:
> "There has been some debate over who introduced 'the passing game' to association football, with the Royal Engineers and Sheffield teams sometimes being credited along with the Scots... Reports dating between March 1875 and March 1880, and including several matches between Queen's Park (and other Glasgow teams and clubs) and the Royal Engineers, Sheffield, and the leading English team of this period – the Wanderers – show clearly that the Scots were [sic] the pioneers here"

### The Evidence Challenge

**Wilson, Stocks, Wood, and Clarke (2025)**: "These statements, however, would appear to be inaccurate"

**Contemporary Reports**: Show passing as part of Sheffield game of football in:
- **1865** (confirmed)
- Possibly **1861** (early indication)

**Implication**: Sheffield passing predates 1872 Scotland-England match by **7-11 years**

## What Is Passing?

### Modern Definition

**Technical Definition**:
> "A pass is an event where the player in possession of the ball kicks the ball to a teammate with the intention that the receiver will be able to control the ball and thus obtain possession"

### Legal Status in Association Football

**Critical Point**: It is **legal** under laws of association football to NOT pass the ball

**Example**: If player received ball, dribbled full length of pitch, and scored without single pass:
- Would NOT be penalized
- Goal would stand

**Famous Example**: Diego Maradona's "Goal of the Century" (1986 World Cup)
- Argentina vs England quarter-final
- Went past seven England players
- Made **no passes**
- Completely legitimate goal
- (In stark contrast to his "hand of god" goal earlier in same game)

## The "Dribbling Game" Era (1850s-1860s)

### Dribbling as the Proper Way to Play

**Mid- to Late 19th Century**: Dribbling individually considered appropriate way to play football

**"Dribbling Game"**: Term used to distinguish from "carrying game" (rugby football)

### Charles Alcock's Definition (FA Secretary)

**Dribbling Defined**:
> "Manoeuvring the ball with the foot"

**Advice on Practicing**:
> "Keep it as close to you as possible, for then you have more command over its movements than when you allow it to get beyond reach"

### The Pack System

**Typical Pattern**:
- Player dribbling ball
- Followed by teammates in close pack
- Ball only "delegated" or passed when in danger of being lost to opposition

### The Amateur Ethos

**Football as Pastime**: For many gentlemen amateurs, result less important than personal enjoyment

**Hon. A. Lyttleton's Explanation**: Why he didn't pass to Billy Mosforth:
> "Because I was playing for my own pleasure"

**Implication**: Dribbling provided individual enjoyment; passing was self-denial

## Key Findings (Summary)

### 1. Passing in Sheffield Football (1860s)

**Confirmed**: Match reports of passing in 1860s Sheffield

**Earliest Possible**: 1861 (indication)

**Confirmed**: 1865 (contemporary reports)

### 2. Natural Development of Competitive Territorial Games

**Passing and Combination Play**: Appeared to be natural development of competitive territorial games

**Not Invented**: But organically emerged from game dynamics

### 3. The Pack System Proved Ineffective

**Mid-19th Century Pattern**: Football conducted in pack with individual dribbling

**Discovery**: This strategy proved **ineffective** compared to combined play

**Superior Strategy**: Players positioned across playing field using passing

### 4. Tactical Developments Interlinked with Rule Evolution

**Particularly Offside Rule**: Different in Sheffield and Scotland vs FA (London)

**Sheffield/Scotland**: Less stringent offside rule
- Encouraged passing
- Encouraged forward kicking (sometimes long balls)
- Also encouraged **heading**

**FA (London)**: More stringent offside rule
- Limited forward passing options
- Encouraged dribbling game longer

## Implications for Sheffield Rules

### Sheffield's Pioneering Role

**Not Just Rules Innovation**: Also tactical/technical innovation

**Passing Development**: Part of Sheffield's broader football culture

**Timeline**:
- 1857: Sheffield FC founded
- 1861: Possible early evidence of passing
- 1865: Confirmed evidence of passing
- 1872: Scotland-England international (often wrongly credited)
- 1875-1880: Kitching's date range for Scottish "pioneering"

**Implication**: Sheffield was **7-11 years ahead** of commonly credited origin

### Why Sheffield Developed Passing Earlier

**Hypothesis** (to be explored): Sheffield's offside rule

**Sheffield Offside Rule**: Less stringent than FA
- Allowed more forward positioning
- Made passing more viable tactically
- Encouraged players to spread across field

**FA Offside Rule**: More stringent
- Limited forward options
- Made dribbling in pack more necessary
- Delayed development of passing

### Relationship to Our Historiography Entry

**Confirms Collins' Critique**: Can't determine modern game's origins solely by written rules

**But Also Shows**: Rules DID matter for tactical development
- Different offside rules enabled different tactics
- Sheffield's rules encouraged passing earlier
- FA's rules prolonged dribbling game

**Complex Relationship**: Rules both less important (for popularity) and more important (for tactics) than either school claimed

## The Evolution Pattern (Preliminary)

### Stage 1: Individual Dribbling in Pack (1850s-Early 1860s)

**Characteristics**:
- Ball kept close to individual player
- Teammates follow in pack
- Ball only delegated when in danger
- Personal enjoyment over team success

### Stage 2: Emergence of Passing (Sheffield, 1861-1865)

**Characteristics**:
- Named individuals passing to each other
- Players beginning to position across field
- Recognition that combination more effective than dribbling

**Context**: Sheffield's less stringent offside rule

## Contemporary Terminology

### Terms Used to Describe Passing/Combination

**From Search Terms Used**:

1. **"Combination"**: Working together, but could mean backing up or actual passing
2. **"Useful"**: Player being useful to team (often through passing)
3. **"Worked together"**: Could indicate passing or just supporting
4. **"Scientific"**: Skillful, thoughtful play (often combination play)
5. **"Passing"**: Direct term (when used)

## Summary: The Passing Revolution

### What We Know So Far

1. **Passing appeared in Sheffield by 1865** (possibly 1861) - 7-11 years before commonly cited Scotland origin

2. **Natural development** from competitive territorial games, not sudden invention

3. **Pack dribbling proved ineffective** compared to combination play with players spread across field

4. **Rule-tactic co-evolution**: Sheffield's less stringent offside rule enabled earlier passing development

5. **Scotland may have popularized, not pioneered** passing

### What Makes Sheffield's Contribution Unique

**Triple Innovation**:
1. **Rules**: Created distinctive code (rouge system, less stringent offside)
2. **Organization**: First clubs, first competitions (Youdan Cup)
3. **Tactics**: Pioneered passing and combination play

**Timing**: All in 1857-1867 period, years before FA's development

### For Game Development

**Can Represent**:
- Evolution from dribbling to passing
- Sheffield's tactical innovation
- How rules enabled tactics
- Named Sheffield players making passes
- Specific matches showing combination play

**Historical Accuracy**:
- Don't attribute passing to Scotland exclusively
- Show Sheffield's pioneering role
- Demonstrate how less stringent offside enabled tactics
- Represent evolution as gradual, not sudden
