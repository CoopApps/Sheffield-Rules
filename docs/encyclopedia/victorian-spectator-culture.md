# Victorian Sheffield Football Culture and Spectatorship

## Overview

Mid-to-late Victorian Sheffield created one of the earliest and most vibrant football sub-cultures in England. This entry documents spectator culture, working-class leisure patterns, and the social context of football development in Sheffield between 1857-1890.

---

## Saint Monday and Midweek Football

### The Custom of Saint Monday

**Definition**: Practice of utilizing the first day of the working week (Monday) as a time for leisure activities, with employees often absenting themselves from work.

**Robert Malcolmson's Context**:
> "Work and recreation were commonly polarised" in early Victorian England
> "Recreation was commonly seen as an impediment, a threat of substantial proportions to steady and productive labour"

**Expected**: Popular customs of lower class would be eroded by latter half of nineteenth century

**Reality**: This did not happen with Saint Monday

### Saint Monday in Sheffield

**E.P. Thompson (1874)**:
> "In Sheffield, where the cutlers had for centuries tenaciously honoured the Saint [Monday], it had become 'a settled habit and custom' which the steel mills themselves honoured"

**Thompson's Broader Observation**:
> "Saint Monday, indeed, appears to have been honoured almost universally wherever small-scale domestic, and outwork industries existed; was generally found in the pits; and sometimes continued in manufacturing and heavy industry. It was perpetuated, in England, into the nineteenth – and, indeed, into the twentieth – centuries for complex economic and social reasons."

**Industries Where Saint Monday Observed**:
- Small-scale domestic industries
- Outwork industries
- Pits (mining)
- Sometimes manufacturing and heavy industry
- **Particularly strong in Sheffield cutlery and steel trades**

### Douglas A. Reid's Analysis

**"The Decline of Saint Monday: 1766–1876"**:

**Key Points**:
1. Saint Monday lasted **much longer in Sheffield** as workshop-based observance than elsewhere
2. Main reason for erosion: Introduction of Saturday half-holiday
3. Saturday half-holiday first appeared in Sheffield in **1840s**
4. Leisure emphasis swiftly shifted from Monday to Saturday (in employers' eyes)

**Thompson's Additional Sheffield Note (c. 1874)**:
> Monday was so frequently taken as holiday that large factory owners were eventually forced to treat it as a repair day

**Implication**: If factories closed for repairs on Monday, workers had little choice but to take leisure time

### Football and Saint Monday (1876-1886)

**Research Question**: To what extent was leisure taking precedence over work in Sheffield during late Victorian times?

**Method**: Study how participants and spectators used football to influence this process

**Evidence**: Sheffield and surrounding districts' football enthusiasts playing and watching games in large numbers on midweek days

**Study Period**: 1876-77 to 1885-86 seasons

**Why These Years**:
1. Importance of Sheffield footballing sub-culture
2. Undergoing significant changes (emerging professionalization)
3. **Newspaper match reports considerable** - indicate football activity level

**Newspaper Report Growth**:
- **1871-72 season opener**: Just 1 report
- **1876-77 season opener**: 10 reports
- **1885-86 season opener**: 24 reports

**Shows**: Massive growth in football coverage and activity

### Midweek Football Pattern

**Not Just Monday**:
- Football played on other weekdays too
- Monday remained main focus
- But Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday also saw matches

**Significance**:
- Detailed study of local newspapers (October-March)
- Sports pages reveal interesting patterns
- Amount of midweek football games worthy of analysis

**Original Data**: Study provides new and substantial information on social and sporting habits of football enthusiasts in Sheffield

**Implication**:
- Football became vehicle for working-class leisure
- Challenged employer control over work time
- Midweek matches required spectators to be absent from work
- Shows football's importance to Sheffield working people

## Sheffield's Football Importance in Victorian Era

### Vanguard of Football's Growth

**Nathaniel Creswick and William Prest**:
- Generally credited with formation of Sheffield Football Club (1857)
- **First club outside school/university setting** founded solely to play football

**1860**: Organized fixtures began
- Sheffield FC had local rival: Hallam FC
- Regular competitive football established

**1857-1877**: Sheffield as Football Sub-culture
- At particular points, **one of most important footballing sub-cultures in whole country**
- Before 1877: Sheffield FA promoted own sets of rules
- Separate from Football Association in London

**Sheffield's Influence** (different times):
- Middlesbrough
- Nottingham
- Chesterfield
- Derby
- Wide geographic reach from Yorkshire base

**1877**: Watershed Year
- Sheffield Football Association accepted hegemony of national body (FA)
- End of separate Sheffield rules era
- Integration into national football structure

### Early Professionalism

**Significance**: Clear evidence of early professionalism in Sheffield

**Great Players**:
- John (Jack) Hunter
- William (Billy) Mosforth (also called "Little Wonder")

**Administrators**:
- John Charles Clegg
- William Edwin Clegg (brothers)
- Influential on national as well as local stage

### Historical Documentation

**Existing Histories**:
1. **Richard Sparling** - Sheffield football history
2. **Percy Young** - Sheffield football history
3. **Keith Farnsworth** - Sheffield football history

**Limitation**: None devoted entirely to initial history before 1890
- Each dedicates less than third of book to pre-1890 period

**Recent Work**:
- **Adrian Harvey**: *Football: The First Hundred Years: The Untold Story*
- Informative chapter on Sheffield football

**Research Gaps**:
- Growth in attendance figures
- Type of people who attended matches
- Behaviour of football crowds
- All yet to be analysed in great detail

## Sheffield's Professional Clubs

### Sheffield Wednesday

**Founded**: 1867
- Senior professional club in city by over 20 years

**Honours**:
- **English Champions**: 4 times (last: 1929-30)
- **FA Cup Winners**: 3 times (last: 1934-35)
- **Football League Cup**: 1 time
- **European Competition**: 3 occasions

**Ground**: Hillsborough
- Accorded many FA Cup semi-finals
- **1966 World Cup**: Held 4 matches during Finals in England
- **1989 Hillsborough Tragedy**: See separate section below

### Sheffield United

**Founded**: 1889
- Over 20 years after Wednesday

**Honours**:
- **English Champions**: 1 time (1897-98)
- **FA Cup Winners**: 4 times (last: 1924-25)

**Ground**: Bramall Lane
- Premier Victorian sports venue in city
- Originally three-sided ground, avenue for cricket
- Yorkshire County Cricket held final fixture there 1973
- Winter 1973-74: Enclosed open side with large stand (7,000+ capacity)

**Bramall Lane Historic Matches**:
- **1883**: England vs Scotland (international)
- **1887**: England vs Ireland (international)
- **1888-89**: FA Cup semi-final (Preston North End vs West Bromwich Albion)
- **1911-12**: FA Cup Final replay (Barnsley defeated West Bromwich Albion)

### Merger Proposals

**Calls from time to time**: Sheffield Wednesday and United should merge
- Form one club with prestige and material wherewithal
- Capable of competing consistently on national and European stages

**Likelihood**: Little chance of merger
- Intensity of English football rivalry
- Sheffield as good example of these enmities as anywhere
- Local derby too important to local identity

## Sheffield's Contributions to Football

**Positive Aspects Provided**:

1. **First Football Club** (Sheffield FC, 1857)
   - First club outside school/university for sole purpose of football

2. **First Floodlit Match**
   - Sheffield pioneered night football

3. **Support for FA in Time of Need**
   - 26 February 1867: FA AGM dwindled to 6 clubs
   - Morley considered disbanding
   - Sheffield letter (14 clubs, 1000-1200 members) had impact
   - May have saved FA from collapse

4. **First Inter-Club Match** (26 December 1860)
   - Sheffield FC vs Hallam FC
   - World's first organized club football match

5. **First Knock-Out Cup Competition** (Youdan Cup, 1867)
   - Pre-dated FA Cup by 4 years
   - Inspired Charles Alcock's FA Cup

6. **First Provincial Association** (Sheffield FA, 1867)
   - Larger than London FA at formation

7. **Rouge Scoring System**
   - Differential "minor points" scoring
   - Influenced development of football scoring

8. **Neutral Referees**
   - John Tomlinson (Youdan Cup semi-final and final, 1867)
   - FA adopted strategy for Challenge Cup latter stages (1871)

## The 1989 Hillsborough Tragedy

### Background

**15 April 1989**: FA Cup semi-final
- Liverpool vs Nottingham Forest
- Hillsborough ground (Wednesday's stadium)
- **Thirty-first FA Cup semi-final held at ground**

### The Tragedy

**Deaths**: 96 football fans
- Died in overcrowding at Leppings Lane end of ground
- Early moments of the match
- One of worst sporting disasters in British history

**Regarded as Watershed**:
> "A watershed in the way the game is watched in England"

### Impact

**New Thinking**:
- Construction of English football grounds
- Safety regulations and standards
- All-seater stadiums mandated
- End of standing terraces at top levels

**Ushered in New Era**:
- Safer football spectatorship in England
- For some, perhaps "sanitised" era
- Changed entire culture of attending matches

**Sad Irony**:
> "It seems rather sad, therefore, that a city which has provided so many positive aspects for the game... should be connected with a great spectator tragedy"

### Sheffield's Complex Legacy

**Positive Contributions** + **Great Tragedy** = Complex relationship with football history

- City that pioneered modern football
- City that experienced its worst safety failure
- Both aspects important to understanding football development

## Academic Framework

### Work vs Recreation in Victorian England

**Robert Malcolmson**:
- Work and recreation commonly polarised
- Recreation seen as impediment to productive labour
- Threat to steady work habits

**Expected Outcome**: Erosion of working-class leisure customs

**Actual Outcome**: Many customs persisted, including Saint Monday

### Complex Economic and Social Reasons

**E.P. Thompson's Explanation**:
- Saint Monday perpetuated into 19th and 20th centuries
- Not simple matter of employer control
- Workers maintained autonomy over time
- Customs embedded in workshop culture
- Football became vehicle for expressing this autonomy

### Sheffield's Unique Position

**Why Saint Monday Lasted Longer in Sheffield**:

1. **Workshop-Based Industries**:
   - Cutlery trades (centuries of tradition)
   - Small-scale production
   - Master-worker relationships
   - Less factory discipline than elsewhere

2. **Steel Mills**:
   - Even large factories honoured Monday
   - Forced to treat as repair day
   - Accommodation to worker practices

3. **Saturday Half-Holiday**:
   - Appeared in 1840s (early)
   - But didn't eliminate Monday leisure
   - Both days became leisure time
   - Workers gained rather than shifted

4. **Strong Football Culture**:
   - Multiple clubs by 1860s
   - Organized competition
   - Midweek matches accommodated work patterns
   - Or work accommodated football?

### Football as Social Process

**Figurational Sociology Perspective**:
- Not mono-causal explanation
- Multiple interdependencies
- Football both shaped by and shaped working patterns
- Part of broader process of "sportisation"
- Workers, employers, administrators all involved
- Contested terrain of work vs leisure

## Significance for Game Development

### Sheffield's Unique Contribution

**Not Just Playing Rules**:
- Also spectator culture
- Working-class participation as spectators
- Midweek football accommodation
- Integration of sport into daily life

**Football as Working-Class Sport**:
- Unlike cricket (more middle-class)
- Football embedded in workshop culture
- Saint Monday tradition supported attendance
- Workers used football to claim leisure time

**National Implications**:
- Sheffield model spread to other industrial cities
- Saturday half-holiday + midweek matches
- Eventually Saturday became standard
- But Sheffield showed midweek football viable

### Modern Echoes

**Continued Importance of Midweek Football**:
- Wednesday evening matches
- Tradition traces to Victorian Sheffield
- Workers claiming time from employers
- Football as contested social space
